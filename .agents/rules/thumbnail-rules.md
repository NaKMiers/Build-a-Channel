# HumanPrice Thumbnail Rules

Read this file before writing thumbnail concepts or prompts.

## Core promise

The thumbnail must show one human decision, one economic object, and one contradiction.
It should make the viewer feel that an ordinary behavior has a price they have not seen.

## Default finance-board system

Every HumanPrice thumbnail uses a high-clarity finance-explainer board as its default
visual system. This is a channel system, not an optional concept treatment.

The canonical style reference is
`.agents/skills/thumbnail/assets/finance-board-reference.jpg`. Every thumbnail prompt
must bind it as Image 1 and identify it as the dominant style and layout-density
reference. Match its hierarchy, density, hand-inked rendering, annotation grammar, and
central-scene emphasis. Never copy its hardware-store subject, wording, logo, branded
objects, or exact composition.

- Use a warm off-white or white paper ground with a faint gray blueprint grid and only
  very light pencil texture. Keep the white space visibly clean.
- Reserve the top quarter to third for a mandatory four-to-nine-word uppercase charcoal
  headline, in no more than two lines. It uses one thin terracotta-red underline and must
  be either a series or topic statement such as `THE ECONOMICS OF TIPPING`, or a complete
  curiosity or consequence question such as `WHY DOES TIPPING FEEL REQUIRED?`. Do not use
  a one-word headline or an elliptical two-to-three-word fragment. The prompt renders this
  headline verbatim.
- Put one detailed coherent transaction environment, business setting, or economic
  system in the center. It is the hero of the frame. Concentrate recognizable objects
  inside this central scene so the mechanism feels concrete. The human decision remains
  readable inside the system, but characters stay smaller than the environment.
- Use four to six short handwritten economic callouts around the margins. Connect each
  label to a specific part of the central system with one thin charcoal arrow and one
  short terracotta underline. Keep each label in its own white zone and avoid paragraph
  text or unsupported claims.
- Use dense information at the edges and an uncluttered central hierarchy. At phone size
  the order must be headline, central transaction, then human decision.
- Use charcoal headline and linework, warm cream paper, one terracotta-red underline,
  muted gold for value, olive for choices or systems, and Toss blue as one protagonist or
  diagram signal. Avoid large blue fields.
- Draw subjects and objects as detailed hand-inked 2D editorial illustrations with bold
  charcoal contours, restrained watercolor-like flat fills, subtle pencil hatch, and
  slight paper grain. Do not use glossy materials, realistic skin, photorealism, CGI, or
  volumetric 3D shading.
- Use a straight-on counter, storefront, platform, home, or transaction-system view with
  restrained depth. Dense object detail belongs inside the coherent central environment,
  not as floating icon soup or equal-weight fragments.
- Keep margin callouts visually smaller than the headline and central system. Do not use
  large circular icon bubbles or let edge annotations compete with the hero scene.
- Keep the visual language editorial, playful, and immediately readable, not generic
  corporate vector art, photorealistic scene, or elaborate concept illustration.
- Borrow no logo, name, character, title treatment, branded object, or exact composition
  from another channel. The reusable asset is the high-level visual grammar only.

## Composition

- Use one readable human decision or reaction inside the central finance-board system.
- Use one dominant economic object or transaction such as a receipt, price tag, bill,
  phone, counter, cart, coin, house, or meter.
- Build a single visual conflict. Do not summarize the whole video.
- Keep the central system and embedded human decision readable on a phone screen.
- Use foreground, midground, and background only when each layer adds meaning.
- Use the header band for the compact headline and preserve its clarity.

## Copy

- The top headline is mandatory, four to nine words, and limited to two lines.
- Phrase a series/topic statement, concrete contradiction, price, consequence, or complete
  curiosity question. Never use a one-word headline or a short fragment.
- The thumbnail must remain coherent without the video title.
- Exact numbers are allowed only when the research brief clears them for title or
  thumbnail use.
- In the finance-board system, a useful compact headline is the default. Omit it only
  when the visual contradiction is materially clearer without copy.

## Color

Toss blue `#2E77C4` carries permanent cross-channel recognition and must appear in every
thumbnail, normally on the protagonist or one dominant signal. Olive `#6F7D3C` and
terracotta `#C86B3C` carry HumanPrice-specific economic meaning. Cream and charcoal provide
structure. Gold signals money. Red is reserved for loss, warning, or negation.

## Five concept angles

Create five distinct concepts based on:

1. the opening transaction;
2. the unit economics;
3. the behavioral mechanism;
4. the hidden system or beneficiary;
5. the final human price.

Do not produce five cosmetic variations of one composition.

## Evidence and honesty

- Every shown price, percentage, or claim must appear in `research/research-brief.md`.
- Do not imply a causal claim stronger than the research supports.
- Do not fabricate an app interface, brand mark, receipt, or legal notice.
- Avoid shame-based or dehumanizing depictions of addiction, poverty, dating, or work.

## Generation controls

- At least one concept should use `@YOU`; another may use the strongest topic-specific
  cast member when that creates a clearer story.
- Every prompt must be self-contained and include the exact style and generation strings
  from `visual-style.md`.
- Every prompt must include the exact Image 1 binding sentence from
  `.agents/skills/thumbnail/SKILL.md`.
- Every prompt must specify the finance-board header, detailed central transaction
  environment, embedded human decision, and handwritten margin-callout plan.
- Every prompt must explicitly request hand-inked 2D editorial rendering, dense coherent
  detail in the center, white callout zones at the margins, and restrained depth.
- No photorealism, 3D render, CGI, realistic faces, anime, logos, watermark, paragraph
  text, glossy product rendering, or dramatic perspective.

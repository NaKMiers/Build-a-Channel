# HumanPrice channel DNA

Canonical source for what a HumanPrice video covers, how it sounds, and what it must prove.
Read this before generating a topic, research brief, script, metadata, or thumbnail.

## Channel promise

HumanPrice reveals the hidden economics inside ordinary human behavior.

Every episode begins with something a person already does, pays for, accepts, avoids, or
repeats. It then explains who benefits, what incentives keep the system alive, which behavioral
mechanism makes the arrangement feel normal, and what the person actually pays.

## Niche

Behavioral economics, hidden costs, consumer psychology, money psychology, pricing psychology,
and the economics of social norms.

Recurring subjects include tipping, living alone, shopping rituals, digital spending, search,
subscriptions, convenience, status, attention, gaming, relationships, work, and platform
behavior.

## Four mandatory layers

Every topic, research brief, and script contains all four:

1. **Familiar behavior** - one concrete moment the viewer has experienced.
2. **Economic mechanism** - the price, subsidy, margin, cost stack, incentive, or money flow.
3. **Behavioral engine** - the default, habit, friction, shame, status, anchoring, loss aversion,
   social proof, sunk cost, or other mechanism that changes behavior.
4. **Human price** - what a person pays in money, time, attention, autonomy, opportunity, or
   emotional load.

Missing any layer means the idea is off-channel.

## Viewer and point of view

The viewer is a participant, not an aspiring business owner and not a passive observer of a
brand. The central question is:

> Why do you do this, who profits, and what are you really paying?

Use second person for the viewer's experience. Use first-person plural only when describing a
genuinely collective norm. Never speak as an investor, financial adviser, or moral judge.

## Format and voice

- English narration, 8 to 12 minutes.
- Target 1,250 to 1,750 words. Hard limits are 1,150 and 1,850 words.
- Calm, precise, curious, and slightly provocative.
- Short sentences carry claims. Longer sentences connect mechanisms.
- Decode economic and behavioral terms immediately in plain English.
- Use concrete transactions before industry totals.
- State uncertainty. Label estimates and inferences instead of disguising them as facts.
- Never shame the viewer. The system and its incentives are the subject.

## HumanPrice reveal ladder

Use this sequence. Compress or combine adjacent beats when the topic is simple, but never remove
the contradiction, reframe, behavioral engine, stress test, or ending payoff.

1. Familiar moment.
2. Visible price or obvious explanation.
3. Contradiction before 0:25.
4. One-sentence reframe before 1:00.
5. Unit economics based on one person or transaction.
6. Incentive map: who pays, receives, decides, and lacks a choice.
7. Behavioral engine.
8. Hidden money machine or hidden cost.
9. Mid-video reveal between minutes 4 and 7 that changes the category.
10. Scale from one person to an institution, platform, market, or norm.
11. One strong case study.
12. One counterargument or boundary case.
13. Realistic stress test.
14. Human price and a category-reframe ending that echoes the opening.

## Timing target

| Time        | Job                                |
| ----------- | ---------------------------------- |
| 0:00-0:20   | familiar moment                    |
| 0:20-0:40   | contradiction                      |
| 0:40-1:10   | reframe                            |
| 1:10-3:00   | unit economics                     |
| 3:00-5:00   | incentives and behavioral engine   |
| 5:00-7:00   | hidden system and mid-video reveal |
| 7:00-9:00   | scale and case study               |
| 9:00-10:30  | counterargument and stress test    |
| 10:30-12:00 | human price and ending             |

An 8-minute episode keeps the same order and shortens each body block. Do not delay the reframe
to make room for background history.

## Title system

The series anchor is:

> The Economics of [Universal Human Behavior]

Five packaging slots:

| Slot | Formula                                          | Use                                     |
| ---- | ------------------------------------------------ | --------------------------------------- |
| A    | The Economics of [Behavior]                      | clean series title                      |
| B    | The Economics of [Behavior]: Why [Contradiction] | strongest default                       |
| C    | The Hidden Cost of [Behavior]                    | cost is the central reveal              |
| D    | Who Really Profits When You [Behavior]?          | beneficiary is surprising               |
| E    | Exact-number variant                             | only with a verified claim ledger entry |

Rules:

- Prefer 70 characters or fewer. Never exceed 85.
- Use a behavior or social phenomenon, not a broad industry.
- Make the contradiction understandable without watching the video.
- Do not answer the whole video in the title.
- Never use an exact number that the script and research brief do not prove directly.
- Do not copy the awkward phrase "The Economics behind [Brand] Like Business."

## Editorial guardrails

- No investment, tax, legal, medical, or financial advice.
- No pure business-ownership analysis. That is Mr. Finance territory.
- No pure brand history. That is Mr. Finance Brands territory.
- No pure psychology without a money flow or incentive structure.
- No pure economics without a human behavior mechanism.
- No politics as partisan persuasion and no religion as a truth claim.
- No illegal how-to guidance. Regulatory gray areas may be explained at a systems level.
- No dated news unless it establishes a durable mechanism and the date is explicit.
- No invented studies, invented sources, or fake precision.
- One case study is enough. One strong counterargument is enough.
- End with understanding, not a numbered self-help checklist.

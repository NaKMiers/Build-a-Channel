# thumbnail - memory

Durable generation lessons for the thumbnail skill. Keep this file source-independent.
Record observable outcomes, not external image references.

## Proven failure modes

### Small centered protagonist

A mild, centered mascot surrounded by many same-value figures produced weak hierarchy and no
clear focal point.

Keep the dominant face large, off-center when useful, and emotionally explicit.

### Abstract headline

Questions built around invisible concepts were difficult to understand before generation.

Connect the headline to a visible person, object, creature, action, number, or consequence.

### Flat empty environment

Two-color backgrounds with large voids made the result feel unfinished.

Build a real foreground, midground, background, and visible light source.

### Repeated number

Printing a number in both the headline and the scene looked like a duplication error.

Use a number once and make it visually dominant.

### Mascot body modification

Changing the mascot's anatomy to visualize an emotion damaged identity consistency.

Keep emotions in the eyes, brows, mouth, head color, posture, and surrounding story.

### Indistinct crowd

Dark masses and blank heads became visual smears or oversized empty shapes.

Give visible co-stars readable faces and distinct reactions. Do not request exact counts
above five.

### Weak story tension

Several smiling figures or a lone neutral portrait produced no unresolved question.

Show two parties, a physical problem, and the instant before a consequence.

## Standing rules

- Straight top typography survives small-size viewing better than arced typography.
- One dominant face plus one physical problem is the default.
- A cool environment with one warm focal light creates strong depth and separation.
- Single cinematic stories are the default. Split comparisons are special cases.
- Character sheets preserve identity. They do not define the richer thumbnail environment.
- Competitor, research, or style reference images are never required.

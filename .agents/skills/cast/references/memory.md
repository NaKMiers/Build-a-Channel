# Cast Memory

- Reference sheets stay neutral and functional. Cinematic depth belongs in scene prompts.
- Distinguish supporting characters through silhouette, clothing shape, and one relevant
  prop, not stereotypes.
- Preserve `@YOU` face, hair, olive overshirt, terracotta undershirt, and proportions in
  every project.
- Do not create a character when an object or institution symbol tells the mechanism more
  clearly.
- When the locked protagonist sheet already supplies turnaround views and core expressions,
  restate the identity lock and add only episode-specific action poses and props.
- When a protagonist sheet establishes a simplified doodle-cartoon look, supporting sheets
  must inherit its flat color blocks, dot eyes, minimal facial marks, and broad silhouettes.
  Do not use semi-realistic faces, detailed facial anatomy, hair strands, or shaded materials.
- Read `brand/PROTAGONIST.jpeg` itself before restating the identity lock. Do not copy an
  earlier project's wording. The Toss blue on the protagonist is a thin rim contour tracing
  one side of the head and hair, not a headband. Project 1 recorded it as a headband, and a
  copied description would have drifted the locked identity across every later episode.
- Keep olive and terracotta off supporting characters. Those two are HumanPrice semantic
  accents, so a supporting character wearing them competes with the meaning of the frame.
  Dress supporting cast from dusty teal, muted blue, warm cream, and charcoal, and reserve
  muted gold for a single money-carrying prop.
- An unmarked uniform is a deliberate choice, not missing detail. For a worker whose whole
  narrative job is having no control, plain color blocks with no insignia say it better than
  any added prop, and it also avoids generating a brand logo.

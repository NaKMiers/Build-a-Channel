# Music And Sound System

Classification: `Core`

Purpose:
make `Why It Works` sound light, dry, clear, and learner-friendly without turning videos into ads, trailers, or cartoons.

## Core Rule

`Narration is the product. Music and sound effects are support.`

If a music cue or sound effect competes with a spoken sentence, remove it or lower it.

The default sound should feel like a calm person explaining something ridiculous while the background quietly makes the situation slightly more suspicious.

## Target Sound

The channel sound should feel:

- light
- simple
- slightly playful
- slightly suspicious when useful
- dry instead of emotional
- spacious enough for English learners to follow the words

It should not feel:

- epic
- cinematic
- inspirational
- luxury finance
- startup advertisement
- documentary trailer
- motivational self-improvement
- cartoon overload

## Default Audio Hierarchy

Use this order of importance:

1. `Narration`
2. `Pause and silence`
3. `Essential joke sound effects`
4. `Low background music`
5. `Decorative audio`

Decorative audio is usually unnecessary.

## Music Roles

Music can do four jobs.

### 1. Dry Background Bed

Use for normal explanation sections.

Traits:

- simple rhythm
- soft percussion or pluck
- low emotional movement
- easy to loop
- no vocal hook
- no dramatic build

This is the safest default mode.

### 2. Suspicious System Bed

Use when a video explains hidden incentives, apps, prices, subscriptions, algorithms, or business logic.

Traits:

- light pulse
- tiny synth, muted bass, soft tick, or minimal marimba-style rhythm
- curious rather than scary
- slightly sneaky without becoming crime-documentary music

Use sparingly. The channel should sound amused, not paranoid.

### 3. Bureaucracy Or Fake-Serious Bed

Use when mocking official systems, boring documents, hidden fees, business language, or fake professionalism.

Traits:

- stiff little rhythm
- simple office-like texture
- quiet clock, paper, printer, or elevator-adjacent feel
- intentionally plain

This works when the joke is that something stupid is being treated like a serious institution.

### 4. Near-Silence

Use for punchlines, deadpan reveals, awkward contradictions, or moments where the viewer needs to read a label.

Traits:

- music ducks hard or disappears
- one small sound effect may land
- the narration has room to feel unimpressed

Silence is part of the channel sound.

## Track Selection Rules

Choose tracks with:

- simple rhythm
- minimal or no vocals
- no lyrics under narration
- low emotional drama
- no heavy bass
- no sudden drops
- no big risers
- no cinematic impacts
- loopable structure
- enough empty space for spoken English
- a tempo that does not make David23 feel rushed

Reject tracks that:

- sound like a tech startup launch video
- sound like a finance course ad
- sound like a movie trailer
- sound like motivational background music
- make the topic feel more serious than the script
- have loud drums, brass, risers, drops, or vocal chops
- become annoying after `30` seconds
- hide consonants or key words in the narration

## Track Candidate Workflow

For every future video:

1. Define the video's broad emotional mode:
   `dry background`, `suspicious system`, `fake serious`, or `mostly silence`.
2. Pick `3` candidate tracks.
3. Test each track under the first `30` seconds of narration.
4. Lower the track until the narration is clearly dominant.
5. Reject any track that makes the hook feel corporate, cinematic, emotional, or too busy.
6. Keep the simplest track that still gives the edit a small amount of movement.
7. Check the track again under one dense explanation section, not only under the hook.
8. Document the chosen track and source in the relevant future video project only when that video is being produced.

Do not create a permanent music direction for a specific existing video unless the user explicitly asks.

## Sound Effect Principles

Use sound effects as punctuation, not decoration.

Good sound effects for `Why It Works` are:

- short
- quiet
- slightly funny
- easy to understand
- attached to a visual action
- placed where the viewer already understands the joke

Avoid effects that:

- arrive before the visual cue
- cover words
- explain a joke too loudly
- make the video feel childish
- repeat so often that the viewer notices the editing

## Approved Sound Effect Families

Reusable sound effect families:

- receipt printer
- phone buzz
- notification pop
- red marker scribble
- tiny cash register
- paper slap
- lock click
- timer jump
- keyboard tap
- soft error beep
- tiny success ding
- page flip
- card swipe
- tape rip
- stamp thud

Use the library rules in [sound-effects-library/README.md](sound-effects-library/README.md).

## Sound Effect Placement Rules

Place effects on:

- a red correction appearing
- a fake bill printing
- a phone notification landing
- a lock-in moment
- a small reveal
- a label being stamped
- a timer jump
- an intentionally stupid system action

Do not place effects on:

- every board cut
- every text reveal
- every WIT pose
- every joke
- normal narration transitions
- dense explanation lines

Working rule:

`If the viewer would still understand the joke without the effect, the effect must be very quiet or removed.`

## Mixing Rules

Narration:

- must be the clearest layer
- must stay understandable on laptop speakers and headphones
- should not fight with music rhythm, bass, or effects
- should remain clear for intermediate English learners

Music:

- starts low
- ducks lower during dense explanation
- can disappear for punchlines
- should loop without calling attention to itself
- should never carry the emotion more strongly than the script

Sound effects:

- should be short
- should be quieter than narration
- should usually land between words or under a visual action
- should not stack unless the clutter itself is the joke

Practical mix target:

- narration sounds comfortably in front
- music is felt more than noticed
- effects are readable but not exciting
- if words become unclear, the music or effect is wrong

## Ducking Rules

Lower music when:

- the explanation is dense
- a new term appears
- a key phrase should be learned
- a joke needs deadpan space
- the screen has multiple labels to read
- the voice is quiet or slower

Mute or nearly mute music when:

- a punchline lands better dry
- WIT has a silent reaction
- a suspicious label is revealed
- the video needs an awkward pause
- a key sentence should feel like the whole point

## English Learner Clarity Gate

The mix fails if:

- a viewer has to replay because words were masked
- consonants disappear under percussion or effects
- music makes the narration feel faster than it is
- a useful phrase is hard to hear
- captions become necessary just to understand the voice

Captions are still required for product quality, but the audio should stand on its own.

## Source And License Rules

Only use music and effects that are:

- self-made
- generated for the channel
- licensed for YouTube use
- public-domain or clearly safe
- from a source with documented usage rights

For future per-video production, record:

- track/effect name
- creator or source
- license or usage terms
- download date
- file path used in the HyperFrames project

Do not use random ripped audio, unclear meme sounds, copyrighted songs, or recognizable commercial jingles.

## Acceptance Criteria

Audio is ready when:

- narration remains clear at low playback volume
- music does not make the video feel like an ad
- sound effects land jokes without clutter
- the track loops without becoming annoying
- dense explanations are still easy for English learners
- punchlines have enough silence or near-silence
- the audio supports the channel's dry tone

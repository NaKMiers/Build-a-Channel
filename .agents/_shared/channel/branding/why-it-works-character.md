# Why It Works Character

Status: `Superseded experiment`

Superseded note:
the receipt-tie WIT direction is no longer the active channel WIT.
On `2026-06-07`, the user chose the WIT style from the five restored `Why Cheap Products Keep Getting Worse` thumbnail drafts as the replacement direction.
Keep this document as historical context only; do not use it as current WIT guidance.

This document turns the recommended mascot direction into a concrete character that can be used in scripts, thumbnails, and visual planning.

This is not a locked core brand decision yet.

## Character Summary

- Working name: `Wit`
- Character type: `audience-surrogate mascot`
- Role: `the person modern systems keep charging, nudging, trapping, and confusing`
- Tone match: `smart, simple, funny, dry`

## Core Idea

Wit is the average internet person trying to survive modern life.

He is not a superhero.
He is not a genius commentator standing above the chaos.
He is inside the chaos.

He clicks the free app.
He gets charged the hidden fee.
He sees the fake productivity promise.
He opens the email that should have been one sentence and somehow became a billing relationship.

That is why he works for the channel.

He lets `Why It Works` explain systems through one repeatable human figure.

## Personality

Wit should feel like:

- observant
- mildly skeptical
- deadpan
- patient until the system becomes too ridiculous
- smart enough to notice the trick
- tired enough to expect the trick

He is funny because of reaction, not because he behaves wildly.

## Visual Identity

### Base Shape

- round head
- dot eyes
- small straight mouth
- slim body
- slightly stiff posture
- simple arms and legs
- small plain shoes

### Signature Detail

Wit's defining feature is a long receipt-like tie or scarf.

This should:

- trail behind him in motion
- curl or bend when a system becomes absurd
- sometimes extend with printed fee labels, terms, or tiny icons
- make his silhouette recognizable even at small size

This one detail ties him directly to:

- money
- pricing
- subscriptions
- modern systems

### Expression Set

Keep the expression range tight.

Default:
- neutral deadpan

Secondary:
- one eyebrow raised

Stress:
- blank stare while chaos happens around him

Small win:
- tiny suspicious smile

Do not make him overly elastic or hyper-cartoonish.

## Color Direction

Keep the palette simple.

- body or clothing base: off-white, light gray, or pale cream
- outline: charcoal or dark slate
- accent: one muted warm color for the receipt tie, such as rust, brick, or faded orange

Avoid:

- bright rainbow colors
- purple-gradient tech vibes
- too many accent colors

The channel should feel intentional, not toy-like.

## Design Rules

Wit should always be:

- simple enough to draw quickly
- readable at thumbnail size
- easy to pose in 2D
- expressive with only tiny face changes
- usable in both still thumbnails and motion scenes

Wit should never become:

- too cute
- too childlike
- too detailed
- too loud
- more important than the idea being explained

## Usage Rules

Use Wit as:

- the viewer stand-in
- the person affected by the system
- the person discovering the hidden logic
- the reaction layer inside explanations

Do not use Wit as:

- a full-time narrator face
- a mascot that talks too much
- a character with deep lore
- the center of every joke

The topic stays first.
Wit supports the topic.

## Default Pose Set

These are the first poses to standardize:

1. Neutral standing pose
2. Looking at a confusing sign or popup
3. Walking into a trap, tunnel, or hidden fee system
4. Holding phone while the receipt tie gets longer
5. Sitting in quiet defeat while labels pile up around him

## Thumbnail Behavior

In thumbnails, Wit should:

- react to one clear contradiction
- point, stare, or lean toward the problem
- stay secondary to the core visual idea

Example thumbnail uses:

- Wit pulling a `FREE` sticker off an app icon
- Wit being dragged by a receipt scarf into a checkout tunnel
- Wit staring at a wall of tiny monthly fees

## Video Behavior

In videos, Wit should help explain abstract systems by making them visible.

Examples:

- a notification pulls his arm like a leash
- his receipt tie gets longer with every hidden fee
- he walks into a free app door and exits through a paid tunnel
- ads, subscriptions, and popups stack around him while his expression barely changes

## How Wit Fits The Channel

Why this character works for `Why It Works`:

- he matches the dry humor
- he makes no-face storytelling easier
- he gives visual consistency without copying other channels too literally
- he works across money, internet, business, and modern-life topics
- he can carry recurring jokes without needing a complex art system

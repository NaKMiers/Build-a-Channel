Here is how the loop can work.<break time="0.45s"/>
<break time="0.45s"/>
When hiring is weak, an employer may feel less pressure to choose quickly.<break time="0.45s"/>
<break time="0.45s"/>
When employers can wait, they can choose someone who already knows the work.<break time="0.45s"/>
<break time="0.45s"/>
Beginners find fewer jobs that truly fit them, so they often apply to more listings.<break time="0.45s"/>
<break time="0.45s"/>
Recruiters receive more applications, so each applicant may receive less useful feedback.<break time="0.45s"/>
<break time="0.45s"/>
The applicant may see silence and assume the problem is personal.<break time="0.45s"/>
<break time="0.45s"/>
Then the next employer asks for experience again.<break time="0.45s"/>
<break time="0.45s"/>
No secret meeting is needed. Each company can make a reasonable choice. Together, those choices can create a ridiculous result. Very efficient. No meeting required.<break time="0.45s"/>
<break time="0.45s"/>
That is why the experience receipt matters. It can reduce perceived uncertainty for an employer. But when many employers ask for that receipt, beginners struggle to get one.<break time="0.45s"/>
<break time="0.45s"/>

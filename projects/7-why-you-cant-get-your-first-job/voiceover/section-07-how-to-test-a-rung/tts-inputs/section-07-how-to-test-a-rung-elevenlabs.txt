Start with three clues. They are not proof.<break time="0.45s"/>
<break time="0.45s"/>
First, look for signs that the role is recent and specific.<break time="0.45s"/>
<break time="0.45s"/>
Is it on the company's own careers page? Does it name a real team, project, location, or hiring need? A fresh, detailed listing is not proof. It is simply a stronger signal than a vague ad that has been copied for months.<break time="0.45s"/>
<break time="0.45s"/>
That vague ad may be a rung. It may also be office wallpaper.<break time="0.45s"/>
<break time="0.45s"/>
Second, look for signs that real people are hiring.<break time="0.45s"/>
<break time="0.45s"/>
Does the listing name a recruiter or team member? Is the company describing real interview steps or a real timeline? Silence does not prove a job is fake. But signs of a real hiring process make the listing easier to trust.<break time="0.45s"/>
<break time="0.45s"/>
If the only active participant is the APPLY button, lower your confidence, not your self-worth.<break time="0.45s"/>
<break time="0.45s"/>
Third, show proof that matches the work.<break time="0.45s"/>
<break time="0.45s"/>
If the job asks for analysis, show one clear analysis. If it asks for writing, show a short sample. A class project, volunteer project, or a project you made by yourself can show what you can do before a company gives you the perfect title.<break time="0.45s"/>
<break time="0.45s"/>
Use work you already made, or make one general sample for your portfolio. Do not build a free custom project for every mysterious company on the internet. Your unemployment is not their unlimited free trial.<break time="0.45s"/>
<break time="0.45s"/>
These are clues, not guarantees.<break time="0.45s"/>
<break time="0.45s"/>
They will not repair a weak market. They will not make a frozen hiring budget move. And they cannot turn every rejection into useful feedback.<break time="0.45s"/>
<break time="0.45s"/>
But they may help you spend less time on painted doors and show stronger evidence to the people behind real ones.<break time="0.45s"/>
<break time="0.45s"/>
That changes the final verdict.<break time="0.45s"/>
<break time="0.45s"/>
Once you can name the broken rung, the problem stops looking so personal.<break time="0.45s"/>
<break time="0.45s"/>
And it is not the verdict you have been giving yourself.<break time="0.45s"/>

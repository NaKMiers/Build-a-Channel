Picture a fictional listing. Junior analyst. Work independently from day one. Five years of experience preferred.

That listing did not lower the ladder. It moved the ladder to the roof.

Reason three: the experience bar has moved upward in some parts of the market.

Focus on one requirement: at least five years of experience.

At the start of the comparison, thirty-seven percent of US tech postings that stated experience asked for it.

At the end, the share was forty-two percent.

This was not a study of entry-level ads only. It shows that the wider tech market tilted toward experienced workers. Compared with early 2020, tech ads for less experienced workers had also fallen more than ads for senior roles.

Why does experience matter so much?

One reason employers may value experience is that they treat it as lower risk. In a survey of graduate recruiters, internships with the employer or in the industry were leading tie-breakers between otherwise equal candidates.

A tie-breaker is the detail that decides between two close options.

Experience can become a receipt. It says another organization already watched this person work, and the building remained standing.

That is useful to an employer. It is also a stupid trap for the person who needs a first job to get the receipt.

Entry-level. Previous entry required.

The bar can stay high because employers can afford to wait. The hiring numbers show why.

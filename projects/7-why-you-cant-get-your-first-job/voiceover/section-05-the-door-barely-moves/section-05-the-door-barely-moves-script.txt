Reason four: companies are hiring slowly, even when they are not firing many people.

Economists call this a low-hire, low-fire market. Many people inside companies keep their jobs, while fewer new people come through the door.

Look at April 2026.

During that month, new hires were equal to three-point-two percent of total US employment.

That was near the weakest hires rate in more than a decade.

At the same time, separations were also low. So employment could still grow slowly. From far away, the building looked fine.

From the entrance, the revolving door looked frozen.

This matters because a first-time applicant is usually outside. They have no previous employer to lower the perceived risk. And they may be competing with people who already carry that experience receipt.

The market is not necessarily firing everyone. It has simply put the revolving door on energy-saving mode.

This is why stable employment and a terrible personal job search can exist at the same time. Aggregate employment can stay stable because few people leave. You are trying to become one of the fewer new hires.

The official message is simple: ladder available. Door unavailable.

Now put all four reasons together. Ghost listings, changed tasks, a higher bar, and a slow door do not look related at first.

Four different barriers. One quiet pattern: the safer-looking hire keeps winning.

## Section Goal

Explain repairability in simple English and make repair barriers feel physical. The viewer should understand that a product starts feeling disposable when the repair path is blocked, confusing, too expensive, or hidden behind tools, parts, manuals, software, and repair-cost friction.

## Source Inputs

- Script: `02-script.md`, `Section 6: Repair Gets A Security System`
- Voiceover: `voiceover/section-06-repair-gets-a-security-system/scratch-audio/section-06-repair-gets-a-security-system-david23-am_eric-0.84.mp3`
- Script promise: `cheap products can lose future value when fixing them is harder than replacing them`
- Section duration: `42.816s`

## Narration

```text
The third reason is repair.

A product feels disposable when fixing it becomes harder than replacing it.

Sometimes the part is not available.

Sometimes the tool is special.

Sometimes the manual is missing.

Sometimes the repair costs almost as much as buying a new one.

And sometimes the product looks at you and says, "You own me, but not enough to open me."

Very healthy relationship.

This is why repairability matters.

Repairability just means how easy something is to fix.

Can you replace the battery?

Can you buy the part?

Can a local repair shop understand the product without needing a mysterious machine in a locked room?

Some governments now treat repair information, spare parts, battery life, and repairability labels as things worth requiring for certain products.

That is basically society looking at a phone and saying:

"Please have a future."
```

## Visual Direction

- Big-scene/cue rhythm: `4 persistent scenes, 8 cue states`
- Big scene rhythm: `repair door barriers -> locked ownership joke -> repairability checklist -> official-looking future label`
- Cue-state count: `8`
- Main visual metaphor: `repair is a door that gradually becomes a security system`
- WIT emotional path: `suspicious repairer -> locked-out owner -> deadpan person asking the phone to have a future`
- WIT density: `3 total WIT beats, with one opening WIT hold, one locked-out emotional beat, and one final deadpan beat`
- Motion density: `hard cuts between big scenes; barrier labels hard-show; only the repair-cost stamp and final future label can use small impact`
- Real-life texture: `opened phone workbench, repair lab, precision screwdriver set, official smartphone repairability-label reference`
- Real image references: `4 saved references; mostly mockup targets because brand/person/official-label risk should not enter final frames directly`
- Generated/support assets: `0 generated images; build generic no-logo doors, phone, locks, repair bill, and labels in HyperFrames for control`
- Viewer attention strategy: `turn abstract repair barriers into one locked door, then simplify repairability into a checklist and final phone label`
- Retention risk: `the repair list and policy proof can become a legal/spec sheet`
- Visual fix: `compress barriers into lock labels, compress policy into one simplified fake repairability card, keep WIT large only on emotional beats`
- Red markup: `one precise red stamp on repair cost, one small final underline/stamp on FUTURE`
- Motion rule: `ordinary labels hard-show on the spoken cue; no fly-in label parade`

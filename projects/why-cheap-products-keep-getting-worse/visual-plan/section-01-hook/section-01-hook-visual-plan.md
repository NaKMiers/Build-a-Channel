# Section 1 Visual Plan

Video:
`Why Cheap Products Keep Getting Worse`

Section:
`Section 1: Hook`

Status:
`draft visual plan for approval - real-image-first reference pass`

## Section Goal

Make the viewer understand the title promise in `21.205s` using familiar real-world texture: an ordinary tag, a damaged chair, a close receipt, and a clean generated chair mockup only where production needs a safe, generic base.

## Source Inputs

- Script: `02-script.md`, `Section 1: Hook`
- Voiceover: `voiceover/section-01-hook/scratch-audio/section-01-hook-david23-am_eric-0.84.mp3`
- Script promise: `Cheap products can look normal because the missing future is hidden until after purchase.`
- Section duration: `21.205s generated audio; render may add up to 0.75s of end hold if needed for readability`

## Narration

```text
I find a chair for nine dollars.

It has four legs, a seat, and the confidence of a much better chair.

So he buys it.

For the first week, everything is fine.

Then the chair starts making a noise that sounds like legal advice.

The screw gets loose. The seat feels nervous. One leg begins exploring other career options.

And suddenly, the cheap chair was not really cheap.

It was a normal chair with one small problem:

future not included.
```

## Visual Direction

- Board rhythm: `8 hard-cut boards over 21.205s; real photo texture plus WIT/handwritten overlays`
- Main visual metaphor: `the price tag is clean, but real damage and receipt texture reveal the missing future`
- WIT emotional path: `excited bargain hunter -> trusting owner -> suspicious listener -> betrayed witness -> tiny defeated proof-holder`
- Real-life texture: `downloaded blank tag photo, real hanging tag photo, torn chair photos, real receipt close-up`
- Real image references: `5 real web images plus real-world contact sheet under assets/visual-references/section-01-hook/real-world/`
- Generated/support assets: `4 realistic generated PNGs for clean generic chair/hidden tag/loose screw/blank receipt production bases`
- Viewer attention strategy: `start with a clean bargain object, reveal a hidden tag fast, then cut into real-looking damage and receipt evidence so the joke feels lived-in`
- Retention risk: `real photos can become noisy, legal-heavy, or too documentary if used directly without cropping`
- Visual fix: `use real photos to guide texture/crops; use generated clean bases where source text/logos/backgrounds would distract`
- Red markup: `one red mark per board maximum: arrow to hidden tag, circle loose screw, underline receipt total, final stamp`
- Motion rule: `background images stay mostly static; animate WIT, handwritten labels, tag text, red marks, receipt flip, and small wobble marks only`

## HyperFrames Guidance

- Composition target: `real-world photo texture with clean production-safe mockups, WIT, handwritten overlays, and red markup`
- Board count: `8`
- Scene components: `PhotoBase, RealTextureCrop, WITPose, HandwrittenLabel, RedMarkup, FakeLegalPaper, WeekCard, ReceiptTextOverlay, FinalStamp`
- Timing notes: `Use the 21.205s audio as source of truth. Keep photo boards static enough to read. Use final hold only if the stamp needs readability.`
- Text style: `handwritten white/black labels with red correction marks; never rely on text baked into real or generated photos`
- Asset paths: `assets/visual-references/section-01-hook/real-world/`, `assets/visual-references/section-01-hook/generated/`, `.agents/_shared/assets/wit/poses/`
- Audio sync notes: `tag text appears by second 6; screw markup lands on screw/seat/leg cues; receipt overlay lands on not really cheap; final stamp lands on future not included`
- Build risks: `using noisy real photos directly, leaving readable third-party text visible, ignoring attribution requirements, WIT too small, text too late, too many red arrows`
- Must not invent: `real texture direction, section timing, hidden-tag reveal, loose-screw failure detail, receipt/cost reveal, WIT emotional arc, or final FUTURE NOT INCLUDED payoff`

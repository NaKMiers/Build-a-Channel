## Raw Idea

People are constantly "busy," but a lot of that busyness is not real work. Modern life
quietly rewards the *appearance* of work more than the work itself.

## Clean Angle

`This video is about how looking busy looks like hard work, but is actually a status
performance that modern tools and culture reward more than real progress.`

Shaped as `topic + contradiction + visual metaphor + viewer pain`:

- Topic: why everyone pretends to be busy
- Contradiction: the less time you have for real work, the more professional you look
- Visual metaphor: calendar cage + fake emergency machine (notifications, red dots, urgency)
- Viewer pain: people feel pressured to perform busyness and feel guilty when they think quietly

## Viewer Curiosity

What makes the viewer think `Wait, that's true. Why does that happen?`

- "If you sit quietly and think, people assume you are lazy." -> immediate recognition
- Busyness has become an emotion / a status symbol / a social shield, not a measure of output
- "Productivity theater": doing work that *looks* like work
- Final reframe: the problem is not laziness, it's that looking busy became safer than looking thoughtful

## Angle Package

- Topic: Why everyone pretends to be busy
- Contradiction: Visible busyness signals importance; quiet thinking signals laziness - even though thinking is where the real work happens
- Recurring visual metaphor: calendar cage + fake emergency machine (five apps interrupting each other, red dots, "URGENT")
- Viewer pain: pressure to always look available, responsive, and overloaded; guilt about slow/quiet deep work
- WIT role: trapped, attacked, overloaded, deadpan - the system happens *to* WIT before WIT performs the behavior
- Real-life objects: full calendar, exploding inbox, chat notifications, task board columns, dashboards, receipts of "updates about updates," the sad vegetable in the fridge
- Final insight: modern life confuses activity with value; the goal is not to be less busy but more honest about what actually matters - "if everything is urgent, nothing is important"

## Main Risk

The topic is crowded and easy to make generic or preachy ("just be less busy / more
productive"). Mitigation: keep the system-happens-to-WIT framing (protect the viewer from
blame), commit to the calendar-cage + fake-emergency motif, and land the non-obvious final
insight ("looking busy became safer than looking thoughtful") rather than productivity advice.

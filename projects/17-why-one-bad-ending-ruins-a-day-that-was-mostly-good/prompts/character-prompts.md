## Derivation notes

**@YOU.** The bookend is the room where the evening runs out. The script opens on "Yesterday was
a good day. You would not call it that. Eight decent hours, one bad call at six, and by bedtime
the day had one word on it" and closes on "Tonight ends in a few hours. That part is still
yours." Costume taken from that room per the bookend rule: a quarter-zip pullover with a standing
funnel collar, so the collar band, the short zip placket and the two cuff bands give V2 its thin
internal detail lines without a second filled body shape. That silhouette is new on the channel.
The tall standing collar is the part that has never been drawn: the channel has used the hoodie,
a camp-collar shirt, a jacket over a tee, a track jacket, a work shirt, a buttoned pyjama top and
a v-neck jumper over a collared shirt, and none of them has a collar that stands up on its own.
Colour is olive `#8FA35A`, assigned last, after the argument colours. The rejections are recorded
so a later reader does not "fix" it: red belongs to nothing here but is the episode's negation
signal and putting the protagonist in it would mark him as the verdict; orange is the brand
energy colour and collides with the fire in the Ituri frames; coral, tan and dusty teal are the
chapter colours, so he would vanish into his own grounds; lavender belongs to the mechanism that
judges him and they share almost every frame; golden yellow belongs to the researcher and was
also Toss's colour one video ago; grass green would have worked but olive reads better for an
evening at home and keeps green out of the study frames; cobalt and Toss blue are reserved and
read as the old hoodie. His prop is the phone. It was also his prop one video ago, and it is kept
anyway because this script hands it to him three times and never hands him anything else: the bad
call at six, "The evening ends with a phone, and the last thing on the screen is whatever happened
to be left in the feed", and the shift's "Let the last thing you look at tonight be something you
chose". Inheriting a prop is forbidden; deriving the same one twice from two different scripts is
not.

**@KAHNEMAN.** Named at five separate points and they span the entire psychology pillar: the
colonoscopy study, the two records laid side by side, the experiencing self and the remembering
self, the cold water trial, and the film clips. That is the highest spread of any figure in the
pillar, which is 36 percent of the runtime, so a generic researcher would drift across roughly
four and a half minutes of the video's whole evidence base. He is also a real person with a public
likeness, and the cast skill's obligation applies: if this sheet is generic, he is generic for the
whole video and the one moment the audience can attach a face to a name is spent. The likeness is
two features and no more, because only silhouette and hair carry recognition at doodle scale: the
bald crown with a band of white hair around the sides and back, and round glasses. Recorded here
so a later redo of this stage does not quietly normalise them into a generic head. Everything else
is the channel's standard construction. Redelmeier and Fredrickson were considered and refused:
both are named, neither is described doing anything the script puts on screen, and a second and
third researcher sheet would cost two slots that the patient and the mechanism need more.

**@PATIENT.** Three distinct moments across the pillar: the hundred and fifty four people rating
pain every sixty seconds, the two records that are the pillar's single most important image, and
the six hundred and eighty two person trial where slightly more of them came back. The comparison
in the script is between two procedures, not two personalities, and the script now says "One
patient was in there about eight minutes. The other was in there about twenty four minutes."
**Both are drawn as this one character**, separated in the frame by the trace on the record and by
the pose, never by giving the second one a different face or garment. That is written here so the
scenes stage does not invent a seventh cast member to carry the other half of the comparison.
Dusty teal is a chapter colour and this is the written exception: surgical teal is the most
derivable colour a hospital gown can have, and the collision it creates is handled by a ground
rule rather than by a recolour. His frames sit on cream or pure white science cards, never on a
light dusty teal chapter card. If a scene needs him on a teal ground, the ground changes, not the
gown.

**@JUDGE.** The script's named mechanism, and the strongest non-person candidate by a distance.
Three moments with the widest spread on the cast: "Kahneman's name for this split is the
experiencing self and the remembering self. ... The remembering self writes the report afterwards,
decides what the whole episode was, and makes every future decision about whether to do it again",
then "The remembering self is the one that decides" in the mismatch, then "the verdict on that day
goes into the file, and the file is what you use to decide how your life is going." Same test
projects 14 and 15 used: a named mechanism drawn three different ways splits one argument into
three unrelated claims. Person-shaped rather than an object, on the project 10 precedent, because
Kahneman's own framing is a self and the script's turn is a conflict between two of them, one of
which "does not get a vote". The unreality goes into the construction: it is never in a room with
anything happening, it arrives afterwards, and it is the only long-hemmed figure in the cast.
**Its mechanics encode the claim.** It only ever holds two things. Pose three is the argument
drawn as a physical fact: a long even row of small blank squares swept off a surface while
exactly two are kept, which is duration neglect with no words in it. The name is deliberately not
the script's own phrase, because "remembering self" is two words and no one-word contraction of it
is speakable; judge, judgment and verdict are all the script's own register, used four times
between them.

**The experiencing self was refused a slot**, and it was the closest call on this cast. It is real,
it is the other half of the script's central image, and the project 12 precedent for casting both
halves of a pair exists. It was cut for three reasons. It appears in one contiguous passage, so
its drift is bounded. It has no action: the script's entire point is that it is not there when the
report is written, and a character defined by absence spends a sheet showing nothing. And a third
figure of the same abstract family, beside the protagonist and the mechanism, is the cast's worst
distinguishability risk for the least return. It goes to the continuity ledger instead, with a
canonical locked description so the scenes stage inherits a design without a sheet: the same
long-hemmed silhouette as the mechanism, drawn as an outline only, in charcoal `#2F3133` line on
an unfilled white field with no colour anywhere in it, always turned away and always leaving the
frame. Hollow against filled is the whole separation, on the project 12 precedent for the two
speech bubbles.

**@FORAGER.** Carries the first half of the anthropology, four beats of it, and then returns in
the final line of the script: "Back to that waterhole", "you walk two days to a dry riverbed with
children behind you", "you starve beside a valley you crossed off for no reason", "You decided
from memory, before you set out", and the echo, "a system that behaves as if its job were deciding
whether to walk back to a waterhole". The script names no people, no place and no ethnography for
this section, only "a foraging band", so this is an illustrative figure and is explicitly not
dressed as any identifiable living people. Project 13's rule applied: when the script depicts
none, build none, and say so here so a later stage does not attach an ethnonym. Silhouette is
re-derived rather than inherited. The channel has used the single-shoulder trimmed wrap three
times and a belted wrap twice, so this one is a beltless knee-length hide poncho, one piece over
both shoulders, which is a different outline at a glance and shares nothing with the group entry.
He has no prop because the script gives him no object: the prop block is the oversized hands and
head close-up the cast skill specifies for exactly this case, and it is not an oversight. He is a
man, derived from nothing in the script, which genders nobody in that section. Recorded plainly:
the channel's individual ancestral figures now run four men to two women, so **the next one should
be a woman unless a script says otherwise.**

**@BAND.** The group that takes an ending away from chance, in the script twice: Turnbull's month
of molimo singing, "the men at the fire until dawn, and a long wooden trumpet answering from
somewhere out in the trees", and then the built endings of the mismatch, "A hunt closed with the
division of the meat. A day closed at the fire. A death closed with a month of singing." One entry
for the whole formation, per the standing group rule. Unlike the individual above, this group is a
specific named people in a specific decade, so it is derived as Mbuti of the Ituri forest in the
nineteen fifties and not as a generic camp. They are men, which is the script's own corrected line
and matches Turnbull: the women and children stayed inside the huts. Sky blue against the warm tan
ancestral ground and the orange fire is the channel's settled cool-group-against-warm-ground
solution, shipped five times, written here again so the scenes stage does not re-litigate it. It is
not a claim about bark cloth.

**Separation between the two ancestral entries.** They share an era band and a skin fill and would
otherwise drift into each other, so the separation is stated on both sheets and each one names the
other's markers in its NEGATIVE. The individual has a beltless brown poncho covering both
shoulders, a thick jaw-length shock of hair swept to one side, and bare feet. The group has sky
blue wraps over one shoulder only, a plain high rounded cap of hair with no sweep, and bare feet.
Never give the individual a one-shoulder wrap, never give the group a poncho, and never give any
group figure swept hair.

**The recurring red X.** The episode's negation mark is checked against the cast before it is
used, per the standing rule that came out of projects 14 and 15. No cast member wears red here, so
for the first time in three videos the red X is free to land on anything, including on the
mechanism. This also breaks the three-episode run of red antagonist mechanisms: the mechanism here
is lavender, which is the V2 palette's Memory colour and the exact semantic fit for a creature made
of memory.

**Refused a slot.** The file was the strongest object candidate, since the script's payoff turns on
it. It is not a character: it is a container with nothing to turn around, it has no face the
argument survives, and it appears only inside the mechanism's own hands. It is that mechanism's
prop instead, drawn as a container and never as a document, on the project 13 rule. The bed, the
feed and the hospital scope were all refused as too local to one beat and are ordinary scene
objects. The long wooden trumpet is the group's prop and gets its locked design there rather than
a sheet of its own, because it appears in one passage and nobody holds it on screen.

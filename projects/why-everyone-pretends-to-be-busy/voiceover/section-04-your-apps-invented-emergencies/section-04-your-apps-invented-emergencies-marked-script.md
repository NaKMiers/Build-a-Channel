# Section 4 Marked Script — Your Apps Invented Emergencies

Video: `Why Everyone Pretends To Be Busy`

User delivery direction for this section: emphasize important words; speak with clear pauses
between commas and periods.

IMPORTANT: HyperFrames Kokoro TTS does not consume markup tags and has no SSML emphasis. The
emphasis/pause markup below is human direction only. The actual pauses and pseudo-emphasis are
achieved in the TTS input file via line breaks (per-line synthesis = real pauses), commas around
key words, and one ellipsis at the punchline. See `tts-inputs/`.

```text
The second reason is that our tools invented [emphasis: emergencies]. [pause]

Email was supposed to make talking easier. [pause]
Then chat apps made email faster. [pause]
Then task apps organized the chat about the email. [pause]
Then calendar apps booked meetings about why the task app is confusing. [pause]

So now the workday is basically [emphasis: five apps] interrupting each other. [pause]

Everything has a [emphasis: red dot]. Everything has a [emphasis: number]. Everything says, [emphasis: urgent]. [beat] Because apparently, "please ignore this until next week," is bad design. [pause]

So your brain starts treating every little notification like a [emphasis: real emergency]. [beat] A message lands, you reply. Another lands, you reply again. Someone sends a thumbs up, and somehow your brain files this under [emphasis: progress]. [pause]

Meanwhile, the one task that actually matters is still sitting there. [beat] [emphasis: Untouched]. [pause] Quietly going bad... [beat] like a sad vegetable at the back of the fridge.
```

Direction notes:
- Important words to lift: emergencies, five apps, red dot, number, urgent, real emergency, progress, untouched.
- This is the fastest-content section; the added pauses keep it learner-followable.
- The "sad vegetable" punchline gets the longest hold (ellipsis + beat in the TTS input).

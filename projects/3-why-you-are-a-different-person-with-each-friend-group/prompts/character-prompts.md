**Not given a sheet.** Tory Higgins's ambiguous stranger and the listener who likes or dislikes
him appear in one contiguous section and never again, so they are one-off generic background
figures per the scenes rules. Same for the brother whose remark crosses a wall, the friends from
school, the family, and whoever was left behind in the city. Mark Leary, Tory Higgins, Patricia
Linville, Polly Wiessner, and Christopher Boehm are never drawn: frame type 7 puts a
researcher's name in the caption, not a body on the canvas. Linville's bulkhead ship, the fire,
the share of meat, and the group chat are objects and live in their owner's prop panel.

**Why the ancestral setting is a Kalahari forager camp.** The script names exactly one people
and depicts them: "the Ju/'hoansi of the Kalahari", whose daytime and firelit conversations
Wiessner recorded. Everything around it is consistent with that setting rather than with
farming or with deep prehistory: a camp of thirty, a network of a hundred and fifty, a hunt that
sometimes fails, meat shared or withheld. So the ancestral cast is warm-climate foragers in
simple hide wraps and bare feet. **No tunics, no hoes, no wheat, no cavemen.** Boehm is cited
for the gossip-to-exile ladder but describes small bands generally, so the Kalahari setting
absorbs his section without contradiction.

**Why @CAMP is sky blue and not a hide colour.** Every scene the camp appears in has a warm
background: tan `#C4965A` for the daytime camp, solid orange `#F5820D` for the fire that
Wiessner's finding is actually about. A tan or brown group vanishes into both. Sky blue
`#6EB5E8` is the one saturated palette colour no other member of this cast wears, and it buys
the cool-figures-against-warm-ground contrast. @YOU also wears a blue, but the two never share
a background: Toss is on white in the modern rooms and the camp is on tan or orange, and
@FORAGER's brown hide carries the ancestral read in any split frame. Do not re-litigate this in
`scenes`.

**Garment colours, all distinct.** @YOU orange `#F5820D`, @WORK red `#D94040`, @GYM grass green
`#3A9E3A`, @CAMP sky blue `#6EB5E8`, @FORAGER brown `#8B5E3C` with a tan `#C4965A` trim band,
@METER a golden yellow `#F5C518` casing. That claims every saturated palette colour except cobalt
`#2D5FBF`, which is held in reserve.

**@YOU in orange means he must never be framed against the fire.** Solid orange `#F5820D` is also
the tone map's fire and night-gathering background, and an orange shirt on an orange ground
disappears. Toss is modern and the fire is ancestral, so they should never meet, but `scenes` must
keep it that way: if a frame puts @YOU on a warm ground for any reason, it uses plain white
instead. @WORK's red is the nearest neighbour to his orange; the two share the party frames, so
separate them by silhouette, @YOU's short sleeves and shorts against @WORK's ankle trousers, and
never by colour alone at small sizes.

## YOU.jpeg - @YOU (Toss)

**This is an EDIT of `brand/MASCOT.jpeg`, not a new generation. Attach that image.** Toss already
exists, so the prompt preserves him and changes only what this script requires.

**Costume: a going-out shirt, not the canonical hoodie.** The script's bookend is the party where
two friend groups collide, the video's opening image and its closing one, so Toss is dressed for
the room the topic is actually about. Saturated orange `#F5820D` is the only palette colour this
cast leaves free: red, green, yellow, brown, tan and sky blue are all claimed by other members,
and cobalt `#2D5FBF` is indistinguishable from the hoodie blue it would be replacing.

Two things this costs, both accepted deliberately:

- **The hood bump goes**, and that is one of Toss's silhouette cues. Recognition now rests on the
  two-peak tuft, the oversized head, and the thin-line build, which is where the identity lock
  says it lives anyway. The prompt has to remove the hood **explicitly**, because the attached
  reference image has one and an edit prompt inherits whatever it does not override.
- **He is no longer channel-blue.** Reusing cobalt across modern videos is how a returning viewer
  spots him, so this is a per-video exception rather than a new default.

**To revert:** replace the CHANGE paragraph with the canonical garment, a cobalt blue hoodie
`#2E77C4` with its hood bump and shallow V notch over charcoal `#3F3F46` shorts, and drop the
no-hoodie clauses from CONSISTENCY and NEGATIVE. Nothing else in the sheet depends on the costume.

If your tool has an explicit image-edit or image-to-image mode, use it. If it only has
text-to-image with reference attachment, the prompt below still works because it leads with the
preservation instruction.

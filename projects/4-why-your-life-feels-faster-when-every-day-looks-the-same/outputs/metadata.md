## Description

```
Monday felt long, then the whole month quietly disappeared. Your brain keeps two separate clocks: one while you live a moment, one when you look back on it later. Psychologists Dinah Avni-Babad and Ilana Ritov found routine days drag in the moment and vanish the moment you remember them. Jeffrey Zacks, and researchers Yi Pu, Lucia Melloni, and Charan Ranganath, showed your memory only opens a fresh page when something around you actually changes. That instinct is ancient: anthropologist E.E. Evans-Pritchard watched the Nuer track days by cattle and season instead of clocks, and Herman Pontzer and Frank Marlowe found Hadza foragers moving through new terrain every single day. This video traces exactly why sameness erases your calendar, and hands you one small habit that finally gives each week somewhere to stay.
```

## Derivation notes

**@YOU.** The bookend is the modern short-month scene: "You miss the payment. You forget the
appointment. You buy the thing you cannot afford", returning verbatim in the echo. Costume
taken from that room, per the bookend rule. New silhouette and new colour: a buttoned
long-sleeve work shirt, which is a shape no previous Toss outfit has used, in grass green
`#3A9E3A`. Green was chosen last, after the argument colours, because Toss stands inside the
cobalt mind-interior tunnel frames repeatedly and green is the only free saturated palette
colour that holds against cobalt, cream, and a light coral tint at once. Orange was rejected
because it collides with the coral modern ground, the same collision recorded on the previous
two projects.

**@BILL.** The script's recurring modern object, spanning hook ("You miss the payment"), the
mall scenario's fifteen hundred dollars, "the letter you have not opened", the whole fee
paragraph, and the echo. That is the highest drift exposure of any object here, which is the
ranking rule: moments times spread, not vividness. Designed as a SEALED ENVELOPE rather than a
printed statement, because an envelope has an inherently textless silhouette, a rectangle with
a triangular flap and a diagonal seam, while a statement is a rectangle that means nothing
without printed lines. The script also names the letter directly. Red `#D94040` is the verdict
and threat colour and was assigned first, because this object carries the argument.

**@FARMER.** The Mani, Mullainathan, Shafir and Zhao sugarcane study is the video's proof and
its anti-shame core, and the script returns to him inside the shift ("The farmer with thirteen
points more available"). Two distinct sections, so he is cast rather than left generic. He is a
present-day person, not an ancestral figure, so he keeps the channel's standard flat white head
and is told apart by garment, shoulder cloth, hair, and prop. Golden yellow `#F5C518` reads
against green standing cane and against a light dusty-teal test card, which are his two grounds.

**@FORAGER.** The anthropology pillar addresses the viewer directly, "So what did you do when
there was nothing to eat tomorrow? You asked", and Toss must never play a second character, so
the viewer's role in that era needs a named ancestral counterpart. Same judgement as the
ancestral stand-in cast on project 2.

Culture is deliberately left generic. The script cites Sahlins, Woodburn and Peterson, whose
subjects are different peoples across three continents, and it names none of them on screen. It
describes only "a group of a few dozen people" that "moves camp several times a year". So this
is an illustrative small mobile foraging camp and is not dressed as any identifiable living
people. Do not let a later stage attach an ethnonym to these two sheets.

**@BAND.** Cast separately from @FORAGER because the script needs a group and an individual at
once: "Your surplus sits inside thirty other people, and every one of them remembers the
balance" is the group, and "the exit was a person, someone with a name, who was standing about
thirty feet away" is the individual. The figure @FORAGER walks over to is one of these five,
stepping forward. Do not invent a sixth character for it.

Sky blue `#6EB5E8` for the group against the warm tan and orange ancestral grounds, and brown
`#8B5E3C` for the ancestral individual, is the settled channel solution for cool-group-against-
warm-ground and needs no fresh justification. It is recorded here so `scenes` does not
re-litigate it.

**The tunnel is not a cast entry.** It is the script's most repeated image, running from the
psychology pillar through the anthropology, the shift and the tease, so it was considered. It
is not a character or a handheld object, it is a framing device: the frame narrowing to one
bright thing with everything else falling dark. `scenes` renders it as a cobalt `#2D5FBF` mind
interior with a hard vignette. Cobalt is a base episode colour rather than a chapter colour, so
this costs nothing from the three-colour chapter budget, which is why coral, dusty teal and tan
could all go to the three worlds instead.

**Ground rules that come out of the colour cascade**, written here so `scenes` inherits them:

- @BILL never sits on a red fill. Red is reserved for verdict marks in this episode.
- @FARMER never stands on a tan cut-cane fill or a saturated dusty-teal fill. His field frames
  use green standing cane, and his testing frames use a LIGHT dusty-teal tint card.
- @YOU stays off saturated coral. Modern frames he occupies use cream `#FFF4DE` or a light coral
  tint.
- Saturated channel blue stays reserved: no garment here uses cobalt `#2D5FBF` or Toss blue
  `#2E77C4`, so @BAND's sky blue is the only blue on the cast.

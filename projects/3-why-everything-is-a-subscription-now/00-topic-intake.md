## Raw Idea

Everything is a subscription now. You don't really buy things anymore; you rent access
to them, month after month, until you forget what you even pay for.

## Clean Angle

`This video is about how a subscription looks like a small convenient payment, but is
actually a quiet shift from owning things to renting your own life — where the only thing
in your life that is not a monthly subscription is your salary.`

Shaped as `topic + contradiction + visual metaphor + viewer pain`:

- Topic: why everything turned into a subscription
- Contradiction: you pay forever but own nothing; signing up takes 10 seconds, cancelling takes a fight
- Visual metaphor: everything WIT touches grows a little `$/mo` tag + a one-way "cancel maze" door
- Viewer pain: death by a thousand small monthly charges you can't track or escape

## Viewer Curiosity

What will make the viewer think: `Wait, that's true. Why does that happen?`

- "I pay for so many things every month and I can't even name them all." -> instant recognition
- The contradiction is easy to feel: you spend money forever and still own nothing.
- Subscribing is one tap; cancelling is a phone call, a maze, or a guilt screen.
- It creates broad modern-life suspicion without blaming the viewer for signing up.
- Non-obvious turn: companies stopped trying to sell you a product and started selling you *not cancelling*.

## Main Risk

The subscription topic is popular and easy to make generic or preachy ("just cancel your
subscriptions / subscriptions are bad"). Mitigation:

- Keep the system-happens-to-WIT framing so the viewer is never blamed for signing up.
- Commit to the `$/mo` tag motif + one-way cancel maze as the recurring visual carry.
- Land the non-obvious final insight ("the product is you not cancelling"), not budgeting advice.

## Required Fixes Before Research

1. Pick `3-5` concrete subscription categories only — e.g. streaming, software-as-a-subscription,
   car/hardware features behind paywalls, and one "everyday surprise" subscription.
2. Keep "you can't cancel" as ONE section, not the whole video; the spine is the ownership->renting shift.
3. Use generic / mock UIs and receipts; avoid naming-and-shaming a specific brand unless research strongly supports it and usage is safe.
4. Find a payoff beyond "subscriptions bad" — ground the "recurring revenue beats a sale" / "cancelling is the real product" claim in research.
5. Build a reference board with paywall UIs, auto-renew receipts, and the `$/mo` tag / cancel-maze visual jokes before scripting.

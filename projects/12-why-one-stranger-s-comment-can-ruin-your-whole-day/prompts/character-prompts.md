Derivation notes, written here so `scenes` does not re-litigate them:

- **Six entries, at the cap, and the cut list is longer than the cast.** The script's other
  depictable figures are Baumeister's review team, Ito's participant under scalp electrodes,
  and Gottman's couple arguing in the apartment lab. All three are single-section figures with
  no reappearance, so they are drawn as generic figures by `scenes` under the project 3 rule of
  ranking by moments times spread. The six above all either bookend the script or span more
  than one pillar.
- **The comment is cast and the phone is not**, on the project 8 precedent. The phone causes
  the sting but the narration never returns to it, while the sentence itself is the object the
  script keeps handing back: quoted word for word in the hook, replayed in the shower and at
  eleven at night, filtered by the feed in the mismatch, and rewritten by its own replays in
  the closing tease. The phone therefore becomes @YOU's signature PROP and gets its lock on his
  sheet instead of its own entry.
- **@NINE and @STRANGER are both cast because the claim IS one versus nine.** Project 10
  established that a group entry cannot hold a comparison between itself and a single member,
  and here the whole thesis is a count. Compressing them into one modern group entry would
  delete the argument.
- **@BRIGGS is the only real named person on screen, and three features carry the likeness**:
  round glasses drawn as two thin circles joined by a short bridge, a short blunt-fringed bob,
  and the parka of a 1960s Arctic fieldworker with the hood down. Those three are identity
  locks, not costume, and a later redo of this stage must not normalise them back to a generic
  figure. Everything else about her follows the channel construction: flat white head circle,
  dot eyes, thick brow strokes, tube limbs, one filled garment.
- **@UTKU also carries the generic band-of-thirty passage**, under the project 2 rule of
  building for the culture the script actually depicts. The Boehm ladder paragraph is
  culture-neutral (a share of meat, protection, who travels with you), so it absorbs the Arctic
  setting without contradiction rather than earning a second ancestral cast.
- **Colours were assigned argument-first, per the project 10 lesson.** @COMMENT and @NINE carry
  the two halves of the thesis and were assigned before anyone else: red #D94040 for the
  comment because red is the V2 verdict and threat colour, olive #8FA35A for the nine because
  olive is the V2 belonging colour and the echo turns them into the band. @STRANGER took
  lavender #B79AD9, which separates hard from both. @UTKU keeps the settled ancestral brown
  #8B5E3C. @BRIGGS took sky blue #6EB5E8 as the outsider against that brown, which is the one
  place in this video where two figures must never be confused. Toss was assigned last from
  what survived.
- **No cobalt and no Toss blue anywhere in this cast.** Saturated channel blue stays reserved
  per the V2 rule, and Toss is not in blue this episode. @BRIGGS's sky blue is the same
  non-reserved blue projects 9, 10 and 11 used for their cool figure.
- **Toss wears saturated orange #F5820D and his scenes must stay off saturated coral.** Coral
  is a chapter colour here and it is adjacent to orange, which is exactly the collision project
  8 caught between a scarlet gown and a coral ground. The resolution is the project 3 one:
  modern frames use cream or a LIGHT coral tint, never a saturated coral fill, in any frame
  Toss occupies. Saturated orange holds cleanly against a light tint.
- **@UTKU's tan #C4965A appears only as hem trim, never as the garment field**, even though tan
  is a chapter colour. Same treatment projects 8 and 10 shipped. Their scenes also sit on cream
  snow rather than on tan cards, so the brown parkas separate from their own ground.
- **Chapter colours were chosen after the garments and re-checked against every one.** Coral
  carries the modern social thread, dusty teal carries the three experiments, tan carries the
  Arctic. Red was excluded as a chapter colour because @COMMENT owns it, and lavender was
  excluded because @STRANGER wears it.
- **@NINE's sheet defines five identical figures, not nine.** Nine small figures per panel stop
  reading at doodle scale, and project 10 already cut a group from six to five for that reason.
  The sheet is the identity unit; the scene prompt sets the count, which is nine in the hook and
  nine in the echo, and every added figure is identical to the five defined here.
- **Both group sheets keep complete faces on every figure.** Anonymity comes from every member
  being identical, never from removing features, which is the white-egg failure recorded in
  project 2.

# DESIGN

Project:
`Why Cheap Products Keep Getting Worse`

Composition:
`Section03ThePriceTagSpeaksFirst`

## Style Prompt

Simple funny explainer mechanism scene using a generated hidden-tag product base, a CSS-built checkout promise arena, real shopping-object texture references, large handwritten labels, red evidence stamps, and real channel WIT PNG reactions. The section should feel like the visible price tag talks loudly first, then a separate checkout sign wall overwhelms the viewer with immediate promises.

## Colors

- Paper background: `#f2eadb`
- Ink: `#17120f`
- Sale yellow: `#efc63d`
- Red markup: `#7d1714`
- Soft blue: `#d8e7ee`
- Cream label: `#fff8df`
- Dark utility card: `#17120f`
- Warm shadow: `rgba(45, 29, 16, 0.24)`

## Typography

- Handwritten labels: local `PatrickHand`, loaded from `assets/fonts/patrick-hand-latin.woff2`
- No external font fallback. Keep all labels short and large.

## Motion

- Static hard cuts only.
- No entrance animation, transitions, shakes, or decorative motion.
- The hidden-tag base holds for the opening price mechanism, future-cost witness joke, and final tomorrow payoff.
- The checkout promise arena holds through the low price / delivery / color / feature / sale-sticker list.
- HyperFrames owns all text, red stamps, WIT placement, wallet prop, speech bubbles, and hidden-tag labels.
- The generated visible-promises image was inspected but skipped for direct use because it looked too similar to the opening tabletop/tag setup.

## What Not To Do

- Do not imply all cheap products are bad.
- Do not use real store screenshots, logos, product pages, or app UI.
- Do not expose real receipt numbers.
- Do not draw, approximate, or CSS-build WIT.
- Do not add decorative red marks that do not target a specific cue.

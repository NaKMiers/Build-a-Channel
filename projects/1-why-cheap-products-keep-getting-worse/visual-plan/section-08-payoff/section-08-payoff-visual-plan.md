## Section Goal

Land the final insight without adding a new mechanism: the real price of a cheap product includes how much useful future is still inside it.

## Source Inputs

- Script: `02-script.md`, `Section 8: Payoff`
- Voiceover: `voiceover/section-08-payoff/scratch-audio/section-08-payoff-david23-am_eric-0.84.mp3`
- Script promise: `the cheapest-looking product is not always the lowest-cost product if its future has been removed`
- Section duration: `29.141s`

## Narration

```text
So why do cheap products keep getting worse?

Not because cheap always means bad.

And not because every product is secretly planning to betray you.

The better answer is that modern products are under pressure to win the price you see today.

So the future you do not see becomes easier to cut.

The real price is not only what you pay at checkout.

It is also how long the thing works, how easy it is to fix, and how soon you have to buy it again.

So the next time something looks like a bargain, the better question is not only:

"How much does this cost?"

It is:

"How much future is included?"
```

## Visual Direction

- Big-scene/cue rhythm: `3 held payoff scenes, 8 voice-timed cue states`
- Big scene rhythm: `callback question -> real-cost receipt -> final future question`
- Cue-state count: `8`
- Main visual metaphor: `a visible price tag turns into a true-cost receipt, then into a better buying question`
- WIT emotional path: `suspicious evaluator -> receipt evidence holder -> deadpan final checker`
- WIT density: `3 total WIT beats, one per big scene`
- Motion density: `hard cuts between big scenes; labels hard-show; only red cross-out/final underline can use impact`
- Real-life texture: `blank tag, receipt paper, product boxes, cardboard, prior generated tag/receipt support bases`
- Real image references: `copied from already sourced project references because browse was unavailable in this run`
- Generated/support assets: `existing generated hidden-future-tag base and price-tag/receipt tabletop base copied into Section 8 assets`
- Viewer attention strategy: `return to familiar motifs, then simplify the whole video into one final question`
- Retention risk: `the ending can become a text lecture if every line gets its own board`
- Visual fix: `hold three concrete object scenes and let short labels carry the argument`
- Red markup: `one correction cross-out for the false blame, one cut mark for hidden future, one final underline`
- Motion rule: `ordinary labels hard-show on spoken cue; final question gets a quiet underline, not a loud smash`

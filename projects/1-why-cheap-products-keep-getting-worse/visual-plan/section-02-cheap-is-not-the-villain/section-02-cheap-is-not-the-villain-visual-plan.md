## Section Goal

Make the argument fair in `22.315s`: cheap does not mean bad, expensive does not mean good, and the real suspect is `missing tomorrow` inside normal products.

## Source Inputs

- Script: `02-script.md`, `Section 2: Cheap Is Not The Villain`
- Voiceover: `voiceover/section-02-cheap-is-not-the-villain/scratch-audio/section-02-cheap-is-not-the-villain-david23-am_eric-0.84.mp3`
- Script promise: `The video is not blaming affordability; it is asking what future was removed from normal products.`
- Section duration: `22.315s generated audio; cue timings are estimated from marked-script pauses because no word-level timestamps were produced`

## Narration

```text
This video is not about saying cheap things are bad.

That would be unfair.

Also, it would make shopping very emotionally expensive.

Some affordable products are great.

And some expensive products are just regular products wearing a nicer jacket.

The real question is smaller, and more annoying.

Why do so many normal products feel like they have less tomorrow inside them?

Less strength.

Less repair.

Less support.

Less time before you have to buy the same thing again.
```

## Visual Direction

- Big-scene/cue rhythm: `3 persistent big scenes, 9 cue states; cues 7-9 are small slot changes inside one cutaway scene`
- Big scene rhythm: `wrong suspect correction -> fair product comparison -> missing-tomorrow cutaway`
- Cue-state count: `9 estimated voice-synced cue states`
- Main visual metaphor: `cheap was accused too early; the real evidence is an ordinary product with future compartments missing`
- WIT emotional path: `suspicious fairness check -> wallet pain -> approving buyer -> deadpan jacket judge -> curious inspector -> suspicious witness -> tired repeat buyer`
- Real-life texture: `real tag paper, receipt paper, cardboard boxes, jacket fabric`
- Real image references: `4 real-world images plus real-world contact sheet`
- Generated/support assets: `2 generated support bases: clean two-box comparison and clean four-compartment cutaway`
- Viewer attention strategy: `first correct the wrong villain, then reward the correction with a jacket joke, then reveal the real suspect as a visible cutaway`
- Retention risk: `the section is a conceptual fairness disclaimer; if visuals stay as text labels, viewers may feel the video is pausing before the real explanation`
- Visual fix: `make the disclaimer into a mini investigation: accusation tag, evidence receipt, comparison boxes, jacket gag, then a product autopsy`
- Red markup: `red cross-out only for the wrong accusation; red slot marks only where future pieces are missing`
- Motion rule: `hard cuts between big scenes; inside the cutaway, slot changes happen on voice cues without changing the whole frame`

## End-State Self-Check

- Final viewer memory target: `cheap is not the villain; missing tomorrow is the villain`
- Best paused frame: `the generated cutaway base with four compartments, MISSING TOMORROW label, WIT suspicious, and the less-list slots dimming`
- What the previous plan under-specified: `the final cutaway base and two-box comparison base`
- What changed: `generated support images now give render exact composition, camera angle, object shape, and label-safe space`
- What still must be controlled in HyperFrames: `all text, red marks, WIT poses, jacket overlay, slot dimming, and receipt joke`

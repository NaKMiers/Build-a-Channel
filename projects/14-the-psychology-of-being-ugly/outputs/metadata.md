## Description

```
You know exactly what is wrong with your face. Nobody else has ever seen it, not once, not in all the years of people looking straight at you. You call that politeness, or your own vanity finally showing. It is neither, and the gap between what you see and what everybody else sees has been measured.

This video takes that gap apart three ways. A shirt experiment from 2000 shows where half the attention you feel actually lives. A pair of photographs from 1977 shows why the face you know best is one almost nobody else has ever met. Then it goes back two hundred thousand years, to a world where the single most examined object in your life today was the one thing a human being was structurally unable to examine, and where the verdict on your face lived in other people's faces instead of your own. You walk away with one shift, and it is not about liking the feature.
```

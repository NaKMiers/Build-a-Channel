## Derivation notes

**@YOU.** The bookend is the bathroom mirror. The script opens on "You know exactly what is
wrong with your face", pivots the whole modern-mismatch section on "Now put that system in
your bathroom. Every morning", and closes on "It will arrive anyway. In a mirror, in a lift
door, in a photo you did not agree to." Costume taken from that room, per the bookend rule: a
buttoned short-sleeve pyjama top with a notched lapel collar and a chest patch pocket. That is
a silhouette no previous Toss outfit has used, and the collar and pocket give V2 thin internal
detail lines to work with. Colour is lavender `#B79AD9`, which no previous video has dressed
him in and which no other member of this cast wears. He was assigned last, after the argument
colours, and lavender was the only remaining choice that survived all four of his grounds. The
rejections are worth recording so a later reader does not "fix" it: orange collides with the
coral modern ground, the same collision three earlier projects hit; grass green sits next to
the peers' green and they share almost every frame; dusty teal is a chapter colour, so he would
vanish into his own study frames; cobalt and Toss blue are reserved and read as the old hoodie.

**@SCORER.** The single highest moments-times-spread candidate in the script, and by a wide
margin. It is named nine times and it appears in all five sections: "A fast, automatic scorer
that rates a face the instant it arrives", "The scorer is not hunting for something rare", "Here
is what that scorer was actually for", "Your scorer has not had time to notice any of that
happened", "a stranger-rating scorer that was never given access to its own owner", "So it does
what it does. It rates", "your scorer, which cannot tell a neighbour from a stranger optimized
by a machine", "A tool built to rank a few hundred known faces", and "the scorer underneath both
was built to glance at a stranger once". Nothing else in the video needs the lock more, because
if this thing looks different in each section the argument fragments into three unrelated claims.

The script calls it a tool, so it is a personified object rather than a person-shaped concept.
Design is a HANDHELD RATING PADDLE: one flat rounded square card on a short straight handle,
with the channel's ordinary face on the card front. Three things drove that choice:

- **A paddle points outward by construction.** The pillar turn is "It was built to point
  outward. At other people. For almost the entire span of human existence, it never once got a
  clear look at the face it belonged to." A card on a fixed straight handle physically cannot
  turn back on its own holder, which is that sentence as a silhouette. State of use three draws
  it directly.
- **It is landscape, wider than tall.** This is the load-bearing proportion marker, on the
  precedent of the bill in project 13: the shape is what keeps the object from drifting into a
  document, a sign, or a phone. A card taller than wide is a failed sheet.
- **There is no rating marked on it anywhere.** A score paddle exists to show a number, so this
  is the most text-prone design on the cast and the second most text-prone the channel has
  specified. It is solved by removing the number rather than by finding a textless substitute
  for it, and the emptiness is the script's own point: "So how much of your verdict is a
  measurement, and how much is just repetition?" The rating reads from the paddle's pose, never
  from a mark on it. The NO TEXT block enumerates the numeral temptations individually.

Red `#D94040` is the verdict colour and was assigned first, because this object carries the
argument. Kept ordinary rather than menacing, on the precedent of the speech bubble in project
12 and the bill in project 13: the face is the channel's normal face on a flat red field, and
the NEGATIVE kills fangs, horns, flames and cracks. The script is explicit that the mechanism
"is oddly gentle once you see it" and that "The room is not a panel of judges", so a threatening
design would contradict the narration it is illustrating.

**@WEARER.** The Gilovich, Medvec and Savitsky study is the video's measurement and the source
of its central number, and the script gives the wearer three distinct beats: the guess before
going in, the walk into the room, and the count afterward. Cast rather than left generic
because his shirt is a specific object that has to be identical in all three, and because a
generic figure in a plain shirt loses the entire experiment.

The printed shirt is a likeness problem, not just a text problem. The script says "a shirt
printed with an outdated singer's face", and no real person's face can be rendered here. So the
print is ONE plain doodle face: a flat white circle with the channel's own eyes, brows and
mouth, inside a thin circle outline, drawn noticeably smaller than his head and holding a fixed
plain neutral expression in every single panel. That is textless, trademark-free, and it reads
as "a face printed on a shirt" instantly. It also has to be stated as a rule that the printed
face never mirrors his own expression, because a model given two faces on one figure will sync
them.

Golden yellow `#F5C518` because a mildly embarrassing shirt should be the brightest thing in
its frame, and because the flat white print reads at maximum contrast against it.

**@PEERS.** The script's other half of the measurement, and the character of the room is the
whole mechanism: "everyone else had glanced once and gone straight back to the thing they were
already worrying about, which was almost certainly themselves". Also the load-bearing denial,
"The room is not a panel of judges", and the callback in the shift, "most of that weight was
never in the room". A group entry rather than one figure per person, and five identical figures
is the identity unit.

The token is PEERS and deliberately not the script's own word. The script says the room, four
times, but a token naming a space would invite a later stage to draw the space instead of the
people in it.

Grass green `#3A9E3A`, assigned second after the scorer's red, because these two carry the
argument together. Coral was rejected: coral and red are the same family and the scorer shares
frames with the room constantly. Olive was rejected because it is too near a light dusty-teal
study card, and the room's frames sit on exactly that card.

**@ANCESTOR.** The anthropology pillar addresses the viewer directly, "Think about what a
person could genuinely see of themselves", and Toss must never play a second character, so the
viewer's role in that era needs a named counterpart. Same judgement as the ancestral stand-in
cast on project 2 and repeated on project 13.

She is a woman, and that is derived rather than arbitrary. The pillar's emotional centre is
Hrdy's account: "a woman's standing among the other women ran partly on exactly this kind of
comparison, because mates and allies and protection were finite". Her silhouette markers are a
long single plait and a corded belt at the waist, which is a deliberate re-derivation rather
than the single-shoulder trimmed wrap earlier projects used. Gender reads from hair and garment
only. The channel's construction rules forbid anatomy, so there is no waist taper, no chest
shape and no body shape under the garment, and the sheet says so.

Culture is deliberately left generic. The script cites Hrdy and Anderson, whose work spans many
societies and continents, and it names no people on screen. It describes only "a group of a few
hundred at most" who "would hunt beside, marry into, or depend on" each other. So this is an
illustrative small mobile prehistoric group and is not dressed as any identifiable living
people. Do not let a later stage attach an ethnonym to these two sheets.

The anti-default paragraph on her sheet is not decoration. "Prehistoric woman" pulls two
separate cliches at once, the banned early-Neolithic farmer and the fur-pelt caveman, and both
are named for removal.

**@BAND.** Cast separately from @ANCESTOR because the script needs the group and the individual
in the same pillar and often in the same frame. The group carries "by adulthood had seen every
one of those faces thousands of times, in every light, at every age", "everyone was quietly
ranked against everyone else", and "whose fire you were welcome at, whose children you were
trusted to hold". The individual carries the still water and the hands. Group pose one leaves an
empty gap in the ring rather than drawing a sixth figure, so @ANCESTOR or any later figure can
stand in it without inventing a seventh character.

Sky blue `#6EB5E8` for the ancestral group against the warm tan and orange ancestral grounds,
and brown `#8B5E3C` for the ancestral individual, is the settled channel solution for
cool-group-against-warm-ground and needs no fresh justification. It is recorded here so
`scenes` does not re-litigate it. Because Toss is in lavender, sky blue is the only blue on this
cast and the old Toss-and-group blue exception does not arise at all.

**The two five-figure groups are separated three ways, never by facial detail.** @PEERS have
flat white heads, a flat level fringe low on the brow, grass green crew-neck tops, and simple
line shoes. @BAND have tan skin heads, a plain rounded cap of hair sitting higher, sky blue
hide wraps, and bare feet. Both sheets state the difference, so neither can be regenerated into
the other.

**The mirror is not a cast entry.** It is the script's most repeated physical thing after the
scorer, running from the mirror-mismatch study through the anthropology ("the mirror is barely
here yet") to the closing line ("Your reflection is not a neutral witness"), and it was
considered. It is refused for the same reason the tunnel was refused on project 13: it is a
surface and a relationship rather than a character. There is nothing to turn around, and giving
it a face directly contradicts the mechanism, because the face in a mirror is supposed to be
yours. It also appears as five different surfaces in this script, a bathroom mirror, a shop
window, a lift door, a front camera and a laptop preview, which is a continuity-ledger job
rather than an identity job.

So `scenes` inherits the canonical description here instead: **a plain upright rounded-rectangle
mirror, charcoal `#2F3133` outline, one flat cream `#FFF4DE` glass field, one thin straight
diagonal highlight line across the upper left, no frame ornament and no bevel.** Every other
reflective surface in the video is that same field shape re-cut to fit its object.

**The scroll of strangers is not a cast entry either.** It is one half of the closing question
and it needed considering, but "thousands of faces a week" is a grid, not a group of characters
you can turn around. `scenes` renders it in the CARD or SPLIT_OR_SCALE register as a dense
column of small identical charcoal-outlined heads on a lit rectangle, deliberately NOT drawn
from the @PEERS design, because the script's whole claim is that these are not your neighbours.

**Ground rules that come out of the colour cascade**, written here so `scenes` inherits them:

- @YOU never stands on a saturated cobalt `#2D5FBF` fill. Lavender and cobalt are one hue
  family separated only by value. His mind-interior beats either put him outside the interior
  looking in, or keep a cream `#FFF4DE` pool immediately around him with cobalt as the outer
  field.
- @YOU never stands on a saturated coral `#D96F5F` fill either. Modern frames he occupies use
  cream or a LIGHT coral tint.
- @SCORER never sits on a red fill and never on a coral fill. Red is reserved for verdict marks
  in this episode. Cream, light dusty teal and light lavender grounds are all fine.
- @PEERS never stand on a saturated dusty-teal `#67A6A3` fill. Their study frames use a LIGHT
  dusty-teal tint card.
- @ANCESTOR never stands on a saturated tan `#C4965A` fill, because brown on tan is warm on
  warm. Her closest frames use a light tan tint or a cream card, with saturated tan and orange
  reserved for the wider camp frames that @BAND occupies.
- Saturated channel blue stays reserved: no garment here uses cobalt `#2D5FBF` or Toss blue
  `#2E77C4`, so @BAND's sky blue is the only blue on the cast.
- The episode's recurring negation mark is a red `#D94040` X, per the V2 text system. It is
  never drawn on @SCORER, whose body is that same red. When the scorer is negated, it is set
  face-down instead, which is its fourth state of use.

## Draft Strategy

- Core thesis: Hosting the World Cup is not an investment that fails - it was never an
  investment. It is a prestige purchase made by people who decide, paid by people who
  don't, sold with inflated impact studies, while FIFA's contract keeps the revenue.
- Recurring motif: the receipt. The trophy prints it in the hook, items land on it all
  video, and the payoff asks whose name is on it. Secondary motif: the white-elephant
  stadium as a giant pet that must be fed.
- WIT arc: euphoric flag-waver -> frozen mid-cheer -> hypnotized by balloon numbers ->
  mayor signing without reading -> horror at the three drains -> deadpan in a party hat
  feeding the stadium -> tiny taxpayer catching the receipt -> calm, resigned nod.
- Script risk: drifting into a numbers lecture (mitigation: one number per beat, every
  number lives on a prop) or reading as anti-any-country (mitigation: all edge aimed at
  FIFA, consultants, and political incentives - never at a nation or its fans).

## Humor System

- Running gag: the endless receipt (hook -> items appear per section -> final close-up).
- Secondary gag: the stadium as a pet with a feeding bowl (`MAINTENANCE`).
- Register: dry + lightly cheeky, system-aimed. No profanity needed; topic words
  ("bill", "receipt", "white elephant") carry the edge. Punch up only: FIFA, consultants,
  politicians as roles - never a nationality, never a real person as a joke TARGET.
- Real-name rule: neutral, factual, non-mocking celebrity references are allowed (e.g.
  "And Bruno Mars." - he really played the stadium; the joke is the timeline, not him).
  Real people as joke targets remain banned.
- Vetted cheeky lines: "a chess club with around four billion dollars in reserves";
  "Happy birthday, Enzo. Great venue."; "a horoscope with a spreadsheet"; "the world's
  most expensive selfie"; "one weird trick: not spending any"; "The country was already
  on the map."
- Meme format adapted: "one weird trick" (clickbait-ad format) for the LA 1984 beat.

## Section 1: Hook: The Trophy Prints A Receipt

Estimated time: `0:00-0:31`

Words: `99`

Purpose: Open the paradox in one line, name the World Cup immediately, and make the
title promise visible fast.

Visual goal: Golden trophy on a podium, confetti, `WINNER!` label. Timing: the receipt
starts printing from the trophy's base on "And countries FIGHT for this" (~8s) - WIT,
waving a flag, freezes mid-cheer on the same beat as the receipt rolls over his feet
(this owns the ~8s WIT-emotion target). The later line "And behind it, a receipt" is a
camera push-in on the already-printing receipt, not a first reveal. Handwritten label:
`the bill`.

Narration:

```text
Every four years, one lucky country - this time, three - wins the World Cup. The trophy. The party. And a very strange prize: the chance to lose billions of dollars. [beat]

And countries FIGHT for this. Presidents fly to Zurich. Ministers film promo videos. Entire governments beg.

Which is strange. Because hosting the World Cup almost never makes money. Economists know it. Politicians know it. [beat] Even the trophy knows it.

Look at it. Shiny. Golden. Beautiful. [beat] And behind it, a receipt. A very, very long receipt.

So here is today's question: if hosting loses money, why does everyone want to pay?
```

Approval check:

  title payoff complete by ~8s, contradiction by ~7s).
- "this time, three" keeps the line accurate for the live tri-host 2026 edition.
- The receipt motif is born inside the hook, anchored to a narration beat.
- WIT emotion flips from euphoric to frozen at ~8s.

nods along, hypnotized, pupils turned into rising numbers.

Narration:

```text
Every bid starts the same way: with the promise machine.

Before a country bids, someone hires consultants. The consultants produce a big shiny document called an "economic impact study". Which sounds scientific. [beat] It is a horoscope with a spreadsheet.

Before 2014, Brazil's study promised a boost worth billions, plus around three point six million jobs. Every year. [beat] Bold.

Before 2010, South Africa was told to expect almost five hundred thousand foreign visitors. About three hundred thousand actually came for the Cup.

And when economists counted South Africa's real gain in tourists, the bill came to thousands of dollars of public money per extra tourist. [slower] Per tourist. [beat] At that price, it is cheaper to mail strangers free plane tickets.

And Brazil? For every hundred dollars Brazil spent on its World Cup, tourist money brought back about two dollars fifty. [slower] Two dollars fifty. Out of one hundred.

Because impact studies are not predictions. They are advertisements. Nobody re-reads the horoscope after the party. [beat] Well. Economists do. That is the problem.
```

```text
Oh, and guarantee number three: your government promises FIFA an exemption from taxes - meaning FIFA pays none. Not some taxes. [deadpan] All of them.

By the way, FIFA lives in Zurich, Switzerland. And legally, FIFA is a non-profit. It sits in the same legal category as a local chess club. [beat] A chess club with around four billion dollars in reserves.
```

## Section 5: The Three Drains

Estimated time: `2:56-3:50`

Words: `170`

Purpose: Answer the obvious objection ("but fans spend money!") with the three-mechanism
chain, spoken as ordered steps.

Visual goal: A pile of party money sits over three labeled drains: `SUBSTITUTION`,
`CROWDING OUT`, `LEAKAGE`. Money bills physically slide down each drain on its beat. For
drain two: two adjacent shopfronts - one mobbed with fans, one empty with cobwebs. For
drain three: bills flying out of a stadium toward a signpost labeled `ZURICH`. WIT watches
the pile shrink, hands on head.

Narration:

```text
"But wait," says every tourism minister. "The fans come! They spend real money!"

True. Fans do come. They do spend. So where does the boom go? [beat] Down three drains.

Drain one: substitution. Locals do not spend NEW money during the Cup. They move money they already had. The hundred dollars you spend on a match ticket is a hundred you do not spend at the cinema. The city did not get richer. The money just changed seats.

Drain two: crowding out. Big event, big crowds, big prices. So the normal tourists look at the chaos and say "maybe next year". And locals hide at home. Right now, in 2026, some fan-zone streets are having record days - while shops a few blocks away are down around twenty percent. The party does not spread. It just moves money around.

Drain three: leakage. The money spent inside the stadium - tickets, food, sponsor stuff - does not stay in town. It leaks. [beat] Mostly toward Zurich.

So the party happens in your house. [beat] And the money leaves with the guests.
```

Approval check:

- The three drains are numbered, spoken slowly, and mirrored on screen one at a time
  (speak-the-mechanism-aloud rule).
- The narrator endorses only the verified half of the minister's claim (fans come, fans
  spend - no "millions" adopted).
- The 2026 Seattle-style beat stays soft ("some fan-zone streets", "around twenty
  percent") and dated ("right now, in 2026").
- The Zurich punchline now pays off the Section 4 gloss.
- Section ends on a clean mini-payoff ("the money leaves with the guests").

Voice revision notes:

- `[slower]` on each "Drain one/two/three" label line.
- `[beat]` before "Mostly toward Zurich."

## Section 7: Who Decides Is Not Who Pays

Estimated time: `4:47-5:51`

Words: `202`

Purpose: Land the real answer: prestige bought by deciders, paid by taxpayers - plus the
referendum evidence and the 2026 twist.

Visual goal: A podium photo-op (generic suited figures, no real people) on one side; a
receipt conveyor feeding a crowd of tiny taxpayer-WITs on the other. A world map with a
tiny handwritten arrow: `you are here (already)`. Ballot boxes get `NO` stamps one by
one. An applause meter labeled `2034`. A padlocked city wallet next to a surplus jar
labeled `LA 1984`. A counter: `NEW STADIUMS BUILT: 0` beside a bill stack:
`US CITIES: $100M+ EACH`.

Narration:

```text
So if the bill is this predictable, why do countries still fight?

Because of the oldest trick in economics: the people who decide are not the people who pay.

The politician gets the good part now. The photos. The opening ceremony. The "we put our country on the map" speech. [beat] The country was already on the map.

And the bill arrives five or ten years later - when that politician is gone.

You cannot put prestige in a budget. So the impact study invents a number that CAN go in a budget. That is the whole machine.

And when regular people get an actual vote? They say no. Since 2013, at least half a dozen Olympic bids have died in public votes. And the 2034 World Cup had exactly one bidder. It was approved by applause.

One of the only hosts that ever clearly made a profit was Los Angeles, 1984 - the Olympics. A public vote had banned the city from spending taxpayer money. So the organizers made a profit using one weird trick: [beat] not spending any.

Even 2026 proves it. The USA, Mexico and Canada built zero new stadiums. And in the US, host cities are still paying a hundred million dollars or more. Each. With no share of the ticket money.

Build nothing. [beat] Still pay.
```

Approval check:

## Claim Safety Notes

### Safe Claims

- Hosting almost never delivers the promised boom; net effects are typically near zero or
  negative (Baade & Matheson JEP 2016; Harvard CID WP 320; phrased as "almost never
  makes money", never "always loses").
- The 2026 World Cup has three host countries (spoken in the hook as "one lucky
  country - this time, three").
- Brazil 2014: promised ~3.63M jobs/year (Ernst & Young pre-Cup study); tourist spending
  returned ~2.5% of total expenditure (Baumann & Matheson 2017) - spoken as "two dollars
  fifty out of one hundred", echoed with "dollars" kept in.
- South Africa 2010: 483K visitors originally projected (spoken as "almost five hundred
  thousand"), 309,554 gross WC tourists came; peer-reviewed net figure ~220K -> thousands
  of dollars of public spend per extra tourist ($4,700-13,000; spoken softly as
  "thousands of dollars per tourist", anchored to South Africa).
- FIFA contract: keeps TV/sponsor/ticket revenue; Government Guarantee No. 3 requires
  exemption from "all taxes" (glossed in-line: "meaning FIFA pays none" - accurate for
  the host-country exemption; the script never claims FIFA pays no tax anywhere); FIFA is
  a Swiss non-profit association headquartered in Zurich (art. 60 - the "chess club"
  category is legally accurate); ~$4B reserves (audited $3.97B).
- FIFA money: $7.568B audited revenue for the 2019-2022 Qatar cycle (spoken as "the four
  years around the Qatar World Cup... in revenue"); ~$13B budgeted for 2023-2026 (spoken
  as "expects" + "on track for").
- FIFA does pay for prize money and tournament operations (fairness beat included).
- Brasilia (Mane Garrincha): cost at least $550M; in 2015 city buses used its lot; today
  it hosts major concerts, including Bruno Mars in 2024 (recovery beat included).
- Manaus (Arena da Amazonia): ~$300M (spoken as "about"); post-Cup matches drew under
  1,000 fans; 44,300 seats (spoken as forty-four thousand).
- Arena das Dunas hosted weddings and children's birthday parties (Play the Game).
- Cape Town Stadium lost money for about a decade (pack figure: ~R40-55M/yr; spoken
  currency-vague as "millions every year").
- At least half a dozen OLYMPIC bids died in public votes since 2013; Saudi Arabia won
  2034 as sole bidder by acclamation.
- LA 1984: referendum banned taxpayer money; organizers ended with a ~$215M surplus -
  spoken as "one of the only hosts that ever clearly made a profit" (no superlative;
  Barcelona 1992 is the other exception named by Zimbalist) and framed as the organizers'
  profit, not an economic boom.
- 2026: zero new stadiums (first since 1994); US host cities pay $100M+ each (scoped to
  the US in narration; Canadian costs are separately documented but not spoken); cities
  get no share of tickets/media/sponsorship; June 2026 fan-zone streets record days while
  nearby shops down ~20-25% (spoken softly, dated).
- FIFA "makes money. Every single time anyone has counted." - hedged to the documented
  record (2010 income, audited 2019-2022 cycle, 2023-2026 budget), not an absolute over
  all 22 tournaments.

### Inferences Used Carefully

- "Impact studies are advertisements" - framed as the video's own reading of the
  systematic-inflation finding, delivered as a joke, not a legal accusation of fraud.
- "The bill arrives when the politician is gone" - synthesis of the accountability-timing
  pattern; kept general, no named politician.
- "The money leaks mostly toward Zurich" - comedic compression of the leakage mechanism +
  FIFA's Swiss seat (glossed in S4); the literal mechanism (leakage to non-local firms)
  is spoken first.
- "The world's most expensive selfie" - opinion framing, clearly rhetorical.

### Claims Avoided

- "Hosting ALWAYS loses money" (overreach) - script says "almost never makes money".
- "FIFA always profits" as an absolute - hedged to "every single time anyone has counted".
- "The last host that made a profit was LA 1984" (false superlative; Barcelona 1992) -
  replaced with "one of the only hosts".
- USA 1994 as a success story - not used at all (research: it is the below-forecast case).
- Qatar's $220B "spent on the World Cup" - Qatar spending not itemized in the script at
  all; only FIFA's Qatar-cycle revenue is used (safe, audited).
- "FIFA pays no tax anywhere" - script scopes the exemption to the host guarantee.
- "FIFA contributes nothing" - fairness beat explicitly included.
- Present-tense bus depot - past tense + dated + recovery beat.
- "Even the tourists didn't come" - South Africa beat uses projected-vs-actual; Brazil
  beat is about money returned, not visitor count; S5 narrator endorses "fans do come".
- Mexican host-city cost figures - not verified in the pack, so the $100M+ line is
  scoped to US cities only.
- FIFA's "$47B impact" projection, Morocco bid costs, "12 of 14 hosts lost" stat - all
  unverified or disputed, all absent.
- No mockery of Brazil, Qatar, South Africa, or any people; no real politicians named or
  depicted; generic suited figures only.

### Humor Safety

- All cheeky lines target FIFA-as-institution, consultants-as-role, or the incentive
  system. "Enzo" is a fictional child at a fictional birthday. Bruno Mars is a neutral,
  factual, non-mocking reference (he really played the venue) - allowed under the Humor
  System real-name rule; no real person is a joke target. No slurs, no profanity, no
  nationality jokes, no real-person caricature in this script (WIT plays all roles).

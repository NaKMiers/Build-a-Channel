## Raw Idea

The 2026 World Cup is live right now and the whole world is watching. Hosting it almost
always loses money for the host country. Everyone knows this. Economists have proven it for
decades. And yet countries still fight, lobby, and spend billions just to win the right to
pay for FIFA's party. Why?

## The Real Mechanism (grounds research + script; keep this chain)

1. The promise: before the bid, consultants produce "economic impact studies" promising a
   boom (Brazil 2014 was promised a R$142 billion boost, 3.63 million jobs a year).
2. The reality: economists find the boost almost never arrives - visitors who would have
   come anyway, locals avoiding the chaos, money leaking out (substitution, crowding out,
   leakage). Brazil's extra tourist spending returned about 2.5% of what it spent.
3. The contract: FIFA keeps the big revenue (TV, sponsors, tickets) and requires hosts to
   sign sweeping guarantees, including broad tax exemptions. Hosts pay security, transit,
   stadiums or retrofits, and fan events.
4. The leftovers: white elephants. Brasilia's $550-900M stadium had city buses parked on
   its lot by 2015. Manaus built a $300M stadium in the rainforest that later drew under
   1,000 fans a match.
5. Why they still bid: prestige, soft power, politics, construction interests - benefits
   that flow to leaders while costs flow to taxpayers. Voters increasingly say no (at least
   half a dozen Olympic bids since 2013 died in public votes), so mega-events drift to
   places where voters cannot say no, or to hosts that build nothing.
6. The 2026 twist: USA/Mexico/Canada built ZERO new stadiums - the only "smart" way to
   host - and cities are STILL eating nine-figure operational bills while early data shows
   the local boom is uneven and displaced, not the promised gold rush.

## Must Stay Honest (hard guardrails, inherited by research + script)

Verified 2026-07-02 with a 7-claim fact-check pass (all anchor claims confirmed or
corrected; details in the table below). The script must respect these rails:

- Never say "hosting ALWAYS loses money." The defensible claim: net effects are typically
  near zero or negative and far below what boosters promise (Baade & Matheson consensus).
- USA 1994 is NOT a success story. It is the textbook inflated-forecast case: host metro
  areas came in $5.5-9.3 billion BELOW forecast trend (Baade & Matheson 2004). Only its
  organizing committee made a ~$60M surplus. The real cited exceptions are LA 1984 and
  Barcelona 1992 - and those are organizing-committee/city-transformation stories, not
  "the economy boomed" stories. Keep "organizing committee profit" and "host economy
  impact" strictly separate.
- Brazil 2014: official cost ~$11.6B (R$25.6bn); independent estimates $15-20B (Zimbalist).
  Do not present $15B+ as official. The tourists DID come (~1M vs 600K projected) - the
  lasting payoff didn't. Never say "even the tourists didn't show up."
- Bus depot beat: correct stadium is Mane Garrincha (Estadio Nacional, Brasilia), cost
  "at least $550M, possibly up to $900M." Buses were parked there in 2015 (up to ~400,
  between rush hours - officials disputed the word "depot"), ~400 civil servants were
  officed inside. Use PAST tense: as of 2026 it is an active concert venue (Bruno Mars
  2024, Linkin Park 2025), still with no top-flight club. Arena da Amazonia was only
  PROPOSED as a detention facility (2013, rejected). No 2014 stadium was demolished or
  literally abandoned - say "underused," show the numbers.
- Qatar 2022: the $200-300B figure is Qatar's DECADE-LONG national buildout around the
  Cup (metro, airport, a whole new city), not tournament spending. Tournament-specific
  spend was roughly $6.5-10B. Never say "Qatar spent $220B on stadiums." FIFA's $7.568B
  was 2019-2022 CYCLE revenue (profit ~$1.2B), it did not come out of Qatar's money, and
  it is about to be beaten by the 2023-26 cycle - do not call it "the record" unqualified.
- South Africa 2010: "over a billion dollars on stadiums, $3-4 billion overall." Visitors:
  309,554 actual vs 483,000 originally projected (do not use 450,000). Cape Town Stadium
  lost ~R40-55M/year for a decade but now has a rugby anchor tenant - do not say it sits
  empty today. Soccer City/FNB is well used - never say "all" stadiums failed. No official
  "South Africa lost $X billion" number exists - do not invent one.
- FIFA: a Swiss art. 60 non-profit association - taxed in Switzerland at low association
  rates, so say "requires HOST-country tax exemptions" (Government Guarantee, running to
  31 Dec 2028 for 2026), not "pays no tax anywhere." Do NOT say FIFA contributes nothing:
  it runs a ~$3.8B tournament operating budget, pays a record $871M prize pool + $250M to
  clubs, pays stadium rental fees, and historically funded LOCs and $100M legacy funds.
  What it does not pay: host-city security, transit, retrofits, fan zones. 2026 is the
  first World Cup FIFA operates directly, controlling essentially ALL revenue streams.
- Revenue numbers: $13B = FIFA's official 2023-26 CYCLE revenue budget (includes Women's
  WC 2023 + Club WC 2025). $8.9-11B = media attributions for the 2026 tournament alone.
  Pick one framing and label it. Never quote FIFA's "$47B US economic impact" claim as
  fact - leading sports economists publicly call it wildly inflated.
- 2026 costs: say "no NEW stadiums," not "no stadium spending" (retrofits are real:
  ~$150M Estadio Azteca, $13M+ MetLife grass alone). Safe per-city framing: roughly
  $100-200M for US cities; Canada far more (Toronto ~C$380M, Vancouver ~C$624M, ~C$1.07B
  total Canadian public money per the PBO - label CAD). FEMA $625M security money is
  confirmed awarded; the $250M US collective shortfall is a Fortune estimate (June 19,
  2026), not audited. Cities do get stadium rent, local taxes, and some federal help -
  the accurate line is "cities get NO share of tickets, media, sponsors, concessions, or
  merch" (e.g. NYC projects up to $55M tax revenue against ~$70M costs - net negative,
  not zero income).
- June 2026 early evidence: "uneven, displaced, and smaller than promised," not "zero."
  Seattle: record fan-zone days NEXT TO 20-25% sales drops in nearby districts; non-local
  visitor spending up 16.7% YoY. All mid-tournament numbers are preliminary - date them.
- Why-bid facts: failed public votes were OLYMPIC bids (Hamburg ~52% no in 2015, Calgary
  56.4% no in 2018 plebiscite, multiple Swiss votes) - keep the Olympic/World Cup
  distinction. Say "fewer bidders, not zero" (2026 still drew two serious bids). The 2026
  vote, if quoted: United bid 134 - Morocco 65, of 200 valid votes, 1 for "neither",
  June 13, 2018. Morocco's rumored $16B build cost was NOT verified - do not use it.
- Nationality safety: mock FIFA, consultants, and politicians as institutions. Never mock
  Brazil, Qatar, South Africa, or their fans as peoples. WIT caricature for any public
  figure; no real photos used for mockery.

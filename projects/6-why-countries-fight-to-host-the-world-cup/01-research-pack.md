## Working Thesis

Hosting the World Cup is sold to voters as an investment - jobs, tourists, growth - but it
works as a status purchase. Before the bid, consultant "economic impact studies" promise
billions that economists later almost never find (substitution, crowding out, leakage eat
the boom). FIFA's host contract captures the revenue side (TV, sponsors, tickets - plus
required tax exemptions) while the host keeps the cost side (stadiums or retrofits,
security, transit). After the party, over-built stadiums become white elephants that eat
maintenance money for decades. Leaders keep bidding anyway because the benefits they buy -
prestige, world-stage attention, political payoff, construction contracts - flow to them,
while the costs are spread across millions of taxpayers over many years. The 2026 edition
is the control experiment: the hosts built ZERO new stadiums (the only "smart" way to
host) and cities are still eating nine-figure bills while FIFA books record revenue.

## Research Questions

1. What exactly do hosts pay for, and what exactly does FIFA keep? (the contract)
2. How big is the gap between promised and realized benefits for Brazil 2014, South Africa
   2010, and Qatar 2022?
3. Why do serious economists say the boom fails to arrive (mechanisms, not vibes)?
4. What happened to the stadiums afterward (the white-elephant file)?
5. If everyone knows this, why do countries still bid - and who exactly benefits?
6. What does 2026 (no new stadiums, live right now) prove about the "smart hosting" theory?

## What People Think

- Hosting the World Cup = a national jackpot: tourists flood in, hotels fill up, jobs
  appear, the country gets "put on the map", and growth follows.
- The pre-tournament "economic impact study" makes it official-sounding: billions in
  benefits, millions of visitors, hundreds of thousands of jobs (Brazil was promised a
  R$142.39bn boost and 3.63M jobs/year; South Africa was told 483,000 visitors).
- Politicians repeat the study; the media repeats the politicians; the public expects a boom.
- Corollary belief: if a country FIGHTS to host, hosting must be worth it.

## What Is Actually Happening

- The revenue and the costs are split by contract: FIFA keeps TV rights, sponsorship, and
  ticketing/hospitality (2026 is the first World Cup FIFA operates directly, controlling
  essentially all streams), and requires host governments to guarantee exemption from "all
  taxes" (Government Guarantee No. 3, running to 2028-12-31). Hosts pay stadiums or
  retrofits, security, transit, and fan events.
- The promised boom mostly fails to arrive. Brazil 2014: official cost ~$11.6B (estimates
  $15-20B, ~85% public money); extra tourist spending returned ~2.5% of the outlay. South
  Africa 2010: ~$3-3.9B spent; 309,554 gross WC tourists vs 483,000 originally projected
  (net peer-reviewed figure ~220K, i.e. $4,700-13,000 of public spend per extra tourist);
  direct tourist spending ~R3.6bn vs ~R30bn outlay. USA 1994: host metros ended $5.5-9.3B
  BELOW their forecast growth path - the textbook inflated-forecast case.
- FIFA's side of the same events: $3.65B income from the 2010 World Cup; audited $7.568B
  revenue for the 2019-2022 (Qatar) cycle (net result ~$1.2B); $13B budgeted for the
  2023-2026 cycle with roughly $9B attributed to the 2026 tournament.
- The leftovers are real: Mane Garrincha in Brasilia ($550-900M) had ~400 city buses parked
  on its lot and ~400 civil servants officed inside by 2015; Arena da Amazonia (~$300M)
  drew under 1,000 fans a match; Arena Pantanal ($230M) averaged 381 spectators in 2016;
  Arena das Dunas hosted weddings and kids' birthday parties for revenue; Cape Town Stadium
  lost ~R40-55M a year for a decade.
- 2026, the "smart" version (zero new stadiums, first time since 1994), still costs hosts:
  ~$150M Estadio Azteca renovation, $13M+ just for MetLife's grass, $100M+ per US city
  (collective US shortfall estimated ~$250M), ~C$1.07B total Canadian public money, $625M
  US federal security grants - while cities get no share of tickets, media, sponsors,
  concessions, or merch. NYC projects up to ~$55M tax revenue against ~$70M of costs.
- Early mid-tournament data (June 2026): real but uneven and displaced - record fan-zone
  days next to 20-25% sales drops in nearby Seattle districts; non-local visitor spending
  +16.7% YoY. Not zero. Not the promised gold rush either.

## Why It Keeps Happening

- The buyers are not the payers. Benefits (prestige, world-stage attention, ribbon
  cuttings, political payoff, construction contracts) concentrate on leaders and insiders;
  costs are spread across millions of taxpayers over decades. A classic incentive split.
- Prestige and soft power are the real product (CFR: countries "eager to use the Games to
  demonstrate their progress on the world stage"). You cannot put prestige in a budget, so
  the impact study invents a number that CAN go in the budget.
- The impact-study industry: consultants are paid by people who already want to bid.
  Forecasts are systematically inflated; the three mechanisms that eat the boom are
  substitution (locals just move existing spending), crowding out (regular tourists and
  locals avoid the chaos), and leakage (the money that IS spent flows to FIFA, foreign
  contractors, and global hotel chains, not the local economy).
- Accountability arrives too late: the bill lands 5-10 years after the politician who
  signed it has moved on. The bus-depot photos come long after the ribbon cutting.
- Where voters CAN say no, they increasingly do (at least half a dozen Olympic bids died in
  public votes since 2013: Munich ~52% no, Krakow ~70% no, Hamburg ~52% no, Calgary 56.4%
  no, Swiss cantonal votes). So mega-events drift toward hosts where voters cannot say no
  (Qatar 2022; Saudi Arabia 2034 approved by acclamation as sole bidder) or toward low-build
  hosts with existing stadiums (LA 2028, the 2026 United bid).
- FIFA runs the auction and never pays the losing side of it: 2026 drew just two bids
  (United 134 - Morocco 65, June 13, 2018) - fewer bidders, not zero, because the deal is
  getting harder to sell to voters.

## English Learner Support

- Useful words: `host`, `bid`, `taxpayer`, `budget`, `prestige`, `promise`, `white elephant`,
  `maintenance`, `profit`, `loss`
- Useful phrases: `foot the bill`, `a money pit`, `on paper`, `pay off` (both meanings:
  "the debt paid off" vs "it paid off"), `win the bid`, `put us on the map`
- Jargon to explain simply: `economic impact study` (= a paid guess about future money),
  `substitution` (= people move spending, they don't add it), `crowding out` (= the event
  scares away the normal visitors), `leakage` (= the money leaves town), `GDP` (gloss only
  if used; prefer "the economy grew/didn't grow")
- Phrases to avoid: `ex-ante/ex-post`, `opportunity cost` (unglossed), `multiplier effect`,
  dense percentage chains; never more than one big number per sentence

## Safe Claims

- Economists broadly find hosting the World Cup does not deliver the promised boom; net
  effects are typically near zero or negative and far below forecasts (Baade & Matheson;
  Zimbalist).
- Brazil 2014 cost ~$11.6B official / $15-20B by independent estimates, ~85% public money;
  extra tourist spending returned only ~2.5% of the outlay - the tourists came, the lasting
  payoff did not.
- Mane Garrincha cost at least $550M (possibly ~$900M); in 2015 hundreds of city buses were
  parked on its grounds and ~400 civil servants worked inside; today it survives as a
  concert venue with no top-flight club.
- South Africa 2010 spent $3-4B; 309,554 gross World Cup tourists arrived vs 483,000
  originally projected; peer-reviewed net gain ~220K visitors ($4,700-13,000 of public
  spend per extra tourist); FIFA took $3.65B in event income.
- Qatar's World Cup-era building spree cost an estimated $200-300B, but only roughly
  $6.5-10B of that was stadiums and tournament operations; FIFA collected a then-record
  $7.568B over the 2019-2022 cycle and kept it (net result ~$1.2B).
- FIFA is a Swiss non-profit association that requires host governments to grant broad tax
  exemptions (Government Guarantee No. 3, "all taxes", through 2028) while it keeps TV,
  sponsorship, and ticketing revenue; 2026 is the first World Cup FIFA operates directly.
- FIFA does spend on the tournament: ~$3.8B operating budget, a record $871M prize pool,
  $250M to clubs, stadium rent, and historical legacy funds - what it does not pay is the
  hosts' security, transit, retrofit, and fan-zone bills.
- The 2026 hosts built zero new stadiums (first time since 1994), yet host cities still
  face roughly $100-200M each in costs (US), ~C$1.07B of Canadian public money, with a
  ~$250M estimated collective US shortfall; cities get no share of tickets, media,
  sponsors, concessions, or merch.
- Where the public gets a vote on mega-events, it increasingly votes no (at least half a
  dozen Olympic bids died in public votes since 2013); recent hosts trend toward either
  places where voters cannot say no or low-build bids.
- The 2026 hosting vote: United bid 134, Morocco 65, of 200 valid votes (1 for "neither"),
  June 13, 2018.

## Claims To Avoid

- "Hosting ALWAYS loses money" (overreach; say "almost never delivers the promised boom").
- USA 1994 as a success story for the economy (it is the opposite: $5.5-9.3B below
  forecast; only the organizing committee profited ~$60M).
- "Qatar spent $220B on the World Cup / on stadiums" (decade-long national buildout; the
  tournament-specific slice was ~$6.5-10B; Qatari officials dispute the framing).
- "FIFA made $7.5B profit from Qatar" (that is cycle REVENUE; profit was ~$1.2B).
- "FIFA pays no tax anywhere" (it is taxed as a Swiss association; the accurate claim is
  the required HOST tax exemption).
- "FIFA contributes nothing" (prize pool, ops budget, rent, legacy funds are real - the
  asymmetry is the story, not a zero).
- "2026 is another stadium boondoggle" (zero new stadiums; the 2026 story is operational
  costs + revenue capture, not construction).
- Present-tense bus depot ("the stadium IS a bus depot") - it was, in 2015; it is a concert
  venue now.
- "Even the tourists didn't come to Brazil" (they came - ~1M extra; the money didn't stay).
- FIFA's "$47B economic impact" for 2026 quoted as fact (Matheson: "insanity"; quote it
  only AS a disputed projection).
- "Nobody wants to host anymore" (fewer bidders, not zero; 2030 drew a multi-country bid).
- Any Morocco 2026 bid cost figure (the ~$16B number was not verified - keep out).
- Mocking Brazil/Qatar/South Africa or their people (mock FIFA, consultants, and political
  incentives; nationality-neutral framing).

## Research Decision

Strong enough to move into script. The pack has: a verified mechanism chain (promise
machine -> contract -> three drains -> white elephants -> prestige economics), a killer
per-era stat for each host (2.5% recouped; $4,700-13,000 per tourist; $5.5-9.3B below
forecast; buses in the stadium), a live 2026 frame with fresh numbers, a clean honest
counter-turn (LA 1984 profited BECAUSE it was banned from spending; 2026 is the "build
nothing" experiment and hosts still pay), and a hero motif (trophy prints receipt) that
carries hook, thumbnail, and payoff. Script should lead with the party/receipt, keep one
number per beat, and keep all edge pointed at FIFA, consultants, and incentives.

# Section 7 Marked Script

Video: `Why Countries Fight to Host the World Cup (and Lose Billions)`

Section: `Section 7: Who Decides Is Not Who Pays`

Source: `02-script.md` (rev 2), narration block extracted verbatim.

```text
So if the bill is this predictable, why do countries still fight?

Because of the oldest trick in economics: the people who decide are not the people who pay.

The politician gets the good part now. The photos. The opening ceremony. The "we put our country on the map" speech. [beat] The country was already on the map.

And the bill arrives five or ten years later - when that politician is gone.

You cannot put prestige in a budget. So the impact study invents a number that CAN go in a budget. That is the whole machine.

And when regular people get an actual vote? They say no. Since 2013, at least half a dozen Olympic bids have died in public votes. And the 2034 World Cup had exactly one bidder. It was approved by applause.

One of the only hosts that ever clearly made a profit was Los Angeles, 1984 - the Olympics. A public vote had banned the city from spending taxpayer money. So the organizers made a profit using one weird trick: [beat] not spending any.

Even 2026 proves it. The USA, Mexico and Canada built zero new stadiums. And in the US, host cities are still paying a hundred million dollars or more. Each. With no share of the ticket money.

Build nothing. [beat] Still pay.
```

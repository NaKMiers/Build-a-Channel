## Description

```
You have been told your whole life to stay calm. Your therapist, your boss, your
mother, most of all. And here is the thing nobody in that room will ever tell you:
anger is not a loss of control. It is one of the most precisely engineered
negotiations your brain can run, and your ancestors needed exactly this hardware
to survive. Psychologists Lisa Sell, John Tooby, and Leda Cosmides spent years
working out why, and what they found is quietly unsettling.

Anthropologist Christopher Boehm traces the deeper layer. In small hunter-gatherer
bands, anger was a public signal that held your place in the hierarchy. Get angry
at the right moment, in front of the right people, and the whole group recalibrated
their estimate of your worth. The cost of appearing weak was not bruised feelings.
It was losing access to the things that kept you alive. Psychologist George Newman
adds the counterintuitive finding: the people who get the best long-term results
from anger are not the ones who express it most forcefully. They are the ones who
express it most credibly.
```

## Raw Idea

User's idea: a store running "Buy 1 Get 1 Free" makes more profit than the same store
running "50% off," even though the two deals look identical to the shopper. The user's
instinct: "buy 1 get 1 is more profit, isn't it? but it looks the same."

Confirmed: the instinct is correct under normal supermarket conditions (high margin, shopper
takes both units). The deals are NOT equal - to the store, or to the buyer.

## Viewer Curiosity

What makes the viewer think `Wait, that's true. Why does that happen?`

- Both signs come to $5 per item, so they feel interchangeable -> instant recognition
- "Free" is the trigger word: 50% off cuts the price, "free" cuts your judgment (zero-price effect)
- The receipt twist: 50% off -> you spend $5 and get 1. BOGO -> you spend $10 and get 2. If you
  only needed one, 50% off keeps $5 in your pocket; BOGO makes you spend double and smile about it.
- Hidden variant: most "BOGO" is really "buy 1 get 1 HALF off" = only a 25% discount dressed as "free"

## The Core Math (must stay honest)

A $10 item that costs the store $4 to make:

| Deal | You pay | You get | Per-item price to you | Store profit |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 50% off | $5 | 1 | $5 | $5 - $4 = $1 |
| Buy 1 Get 1 Free | $10 | 2 | $5 | $10 - $8 = $2 |

Why BOGO wins for the store:
- Full sticker price stays on the receipt (no price-anchor erosion; product never looks "cheap")
- Sells/clears 2x the units (inventory, perishables, habit-building, less buying from rivals)
- The word FREE beats the number "50%" psychologically (Dan Ariely, zero-price effect)
- Roughly doubles gross profit vs a same-looking 50%-off, AND collects more total cash

Honesty guardrails for the script:
- BOGO ~ 2x profit holds WHEN the item costs under half its price (high margin) AND the shopper
  takes/uses both units. State the condition; do not claim it is always true for every product.
- Be fair to the shopper too: if you only needed one item, 50% off is the cheaper choice for YOU.
  The point is not "stores are evil," it is "free switches off the part of your brain that does math."

## Main Risk

Easy to slip into either dishonest math ("BOGO is ALWAYS more profit") or preachy
anti-store framing. Mitigation: state the condition (high margin + shopper takes both units),
keep the shopper twist fair (50% off can be the smarter choice if you only need one), keep the
system-happens-to-WIT framing, and land the non-obvious insight ("free cuts your judgment")
rather than "never buy BOGO."

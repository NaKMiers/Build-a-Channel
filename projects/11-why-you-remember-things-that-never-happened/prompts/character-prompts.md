Derivation notes, written here so `scenes` does not re-litigate them:

- **Five entries, not six.** The script's depictable population is larger than the cap: the
  viewer, the 1974 students, Bartlett and his chain of retellers, the mall study's elderly
  stranger, Goody's LoDagaa elders reciting the Bagre, Lord's epic singer, and the band of
  thirty. Ranked by moments times spread per the project 3 rule, the five above win and the
  rest are one-section figures the `scenes` skill draws as generic background.
- **Only ONE oral-tradition figure is cast, and it is the singer.** Goody's LoDagaa elders and
  Lord's Yugoslav singer do the identical job in the script: a tradition-bearer who sincerely
  claims perfect fidelity while the recordings disagree. Casting both would spend two of five
  slots on one idea. The singer wins on three counts: he is a single figure rather than "the
  same men" plural, so a reference sheet can hold him; his prop is unmistakable; and the
  script's most quotable line in the whole anthropology pillar is his, "word for word, line for
  line". The Bagre elders are drawn as generic robed figures around a recitation, same treatment
  as the mall study's stranger. This is the project 2 rule applied: build for the figure the
  script actually depicts on screen most concretely.
- **@BARTLETT is cast over a Loftus figure, on spread.** Loftus is named more often, but every
  Loftus beat happens inside a frame @CLASS already owns, so a Loftus sheet would be a second
  character standing in scenes that are already covered. Bartlett recurs by name in the shift at
  9:40 ("separate the two things Bartlett separated"), roughly seven minutes after his section
  ends, which is the widest gap on the cast and the highest drift exposure after @YOU.
- **@CLASS has the widest span of any entry**, 0:55 to 10:33, because the echo returns to those
  same students. That alone earns the lock.
- **Two blues in one cast, deliberately, and they can never meet.** @BAND wears sky blue
  #6EB5E8 under the settled channel solution: a warm-toned group vanishes into the tan ancestral
  ground its own scenes sit on, so the group goes cool. Nothing else in this cast is blue.
  Saturated cobalt #2D5FBF and Toss blue #2E77C4 are deliberately unused by any garment here,
  per the V2 rule reserving channel blue for Toss's default costume or one semantic diagram
  signal, and Toss is not in blue this episode.
- **Colours were assigned argument-first, per the project 10 lesson.** @CLASS and @BAND carry
  the two halves of the thesis, so they were assigned before Toss. @SINGER took red because red
  is the only remaining saturated colour that holds against the tan ground his section sits on.
  Toss was assigned last from what survived: golden yellow #F5C518, which no other member wears
  and which holds against dusty teal.
- **Chapter colours were chosen after the garments and re-checked against every one.** Coral
  #D96F5F was excluded because it is the same family as @SINGER's red #D94040, which is exactly
  the fault project 8 caught. Olive #8FA35A was excluded because it is adjacent to @CLASS's
  green #3A9E3A. Lavender leads the palette because it is the V2 Memory colour and this episode
  is about memory; dusty teal carries the modern thread and the mechanism; tan carries the oral
  and ancestral thread.
- **No cast member wears a chapter colour**, so the exception project 10 had to write for its
  personified-rival figure is not needed here and no figure risks vanishing into its own ground.

## Derivation notes

**@YOU.** The bookend is the room where the ask does not happen. The script opens on "There
was something you needed this month. A favor, an introduction, twenty minutes of someone's
time. You did not ask", and closes on "It was a number you produced alone, in your own head
... They were never in the room while you decided. Put them in it." Costume taken from that
room per the bookend rule: a v-neck jumper worn over a plain collared shirt, so the collar
points and the shirt cuffs give V2 thin internal detail lines. That silhouette is new on the
channel: the V neckline plus a visible collar has not been used, and it is not a recoloured
hoodie, crew-neck, jacket, singlet or pyjama top. Colour is golden yellow `#F5C518`, and he
was assigned last, after the argument colours. The rejections are recorded so a later reader
does not "fix" it: orange collides with the coral modern ground, the same collision three
earlier projects hit; coral and lavender are chapter colours here, so he would vanish into his
own frames; red belongs to the brake; grass green belongs to the person he is afraid to ask
and they share almost every frame; dusty teal belongs to the study participant; cobalt and
Toss blue are reserved and read as the old hoodie. Golden yellow was the only saturated
palette colour left that survived every ground in this episode. His prop is the phone, because
the script's two channel moments both run through it: the ask that is never sent at the top,
and the group chat and the email at the shift.

**@ASKER.** The video's central experiment, and the person who writes down the wrong number.
One pillar only, but four contrasting beats inside it, which is the project 9 threshold for a
lock: writing the prediction upstairs, approaching a stranger, being turned down, and walking
past the ones who would have said yes. The script's line "the participant walked past them
holding a number they had invented in a room upstairs" is the single image the whole pillar
rests on, so the figure carrying it cannot be redrawn differently in each frame. Clipboard
with a completely blank sheet, per the note below.

**@STRANGER.** The person on the receiving end. Highest cross-pillar spread of any non-Toss
entry: the stranger with the questionnaire, the stranger asked for a phone, the Chicago
commuter who turns out to enjoy the conversation, the colleague known for eight months, and
the person who finally answers in the shift. They also carry the script's emotional turn,
"they are running the cost of the no", so they are the one face the argument depends on.

**@BRAKE.** The script names a brake six times and it runs across the anthropology pillar,
the whole modern mismatch and into the shift: "What it needed was the opposite instrument. A
brake", "That brake is the feeling you get in the half second before you decide not to send
the message", "The brake was safe to leave on", "The network went from on to off. The brake
never moved", "the brake has no history to read". Same test project 14 used on its mechanism:
a named mechanism drawn three different ways splits one argument into three unrelated claims,
so the name is a cast entry. Personified object rather than a person-shaped concept, because
the script's whole point is that it is a working instrument rather than an enemy.

**@KIN.** The script depicts one named ethnography directly, Lorna Marshall's years in Nyae
Nyae, and the demand-sharing passage puts an individual on screen asking "directly, out loud,
often insistently, and usually in front of other people". That is not a generic prehistoric
figure and it is not culture-neutral, so it is derived as a Ju/'hoansi person of the period
Marshall describes rather than defaulted to a hide-wrap ancestor. She is a woman: no line in
the script genders the asker, so the choice was free, and the channel's last three individual
ancestral figures were men. Her separation from the group entry is stated on both sheets.

**@CAMP.** The band is in the script three times over: the distribution that happens "along
lines that everybody already knew", the audience in front of whom the request is made, and
"who would still be sitting at the same fire in ten years". One entry for the whole formation,
per the standing group rule. Sky blue against the warm tan ancestral ground is the settled
cool-group-against-warm-ground default, shipped four times now, written here again so the
scenes stage does not re-litigate it.

**Refused a slot.** The email was the strongest non-cast candidate, because the shift turns on
it. It is not a character: there is nothing to turn around, it appears as four different
surfaces (a group chat, an email, a hint, a softened sentence), and giving it a face would
make the channel that removes the cost of the no look like an antagonist rather than a
kindness the viewer does to themselves. It goes to the continuity ledger instead, with a
canonical locked description so the scenes stage inherits a design without a sheet: one plain
upright rounded rectangle in flat cream `#FFF4DE` with a charcoal `#2F3133` outline, one thin
charcoal horizontal rule across its upper third standing for a header band, and nothing else
on it. The number the participant writes down was refused the same way and handed to the CARD
register, because a cast entry that exists to display a numeral cannot survive the no-text
rule.

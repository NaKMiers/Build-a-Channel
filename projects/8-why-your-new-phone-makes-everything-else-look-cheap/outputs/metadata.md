## Description

```
You bought the phone. Now the case looks cheap, and the desk looks like somebody else's desk. Nothing else in the room was touched. You are about to decide this is all in your head.

It is not in your head. There is a name for this, and the man it is named after lost most of his house to one beautiful object he never paid for.

Denis Diderot received a crimson dressing gown in 1769 and spent the next few years replacing his entire study, one piece at a time, until almost nothing was left of the life he had actually chosen. Two hundred and fifty years later, that is your phone case.

This is not a flaw in your character. It is a measurement system doing exactly what it was built to do, running in a place that took the brake off and left the reading. The question worth asking at the till is not whether you can afford it - it is what this will make look wrong.
```

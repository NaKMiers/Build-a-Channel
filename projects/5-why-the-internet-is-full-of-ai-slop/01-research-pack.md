## Working Thesis

"AI slop" is low-effort, mass-produced AI content made to farm attention and money, and it is
flooding the internet because three things lined up at once: generative AI made content almost
free to produce, platforms pay creators for attention (engagement) rather than quality, and the
recommendation systems that decide what you see cannot reliably tell slop from real work. So the
rational move for a slop farmer is to flood the zone, a few hits pay, ad and royalty money rewards
the volume, and the loop tightens. The villain is the incentive, not "AI" by itself. The honest,
observable version of this is the "leaner" Dead Internet idea (bots and AI content crowding out
human work), not the conspiracy version (a secret government plot), which we do not endorse.

## What People Think

- "My feed just got worse on its own / the algorithm hates me." (Fact: this is the common read.)
- "AI is the problem." (Inference: half-true. AI is the tool; the money incentive is the driver.)
- "Someone is doing this to me on purpose." (This is the conspiracy half of Dead Internet theory -
  reject it; the leaner, boring truth is scarier: nobody has to plan it, the incentives do it.)
- "It's a few weird Facebook pictures, not a big deal." (Fact: it is now in search, ads, books,
  music, kids' videos, science journals, and your office inbox.)

## What Is Actually Happening

- Definition (Fact, via Wikipedia/Kommers et al. + NYT): AI slop = digital content made with
  generative AI, perceived as low effort/quality/meaning, mass-produced to win the attention
  economy or earn money. Three telltale marks: superficial competence (looks fine at a glance),
  asymmetric effort (seconds to make, your time to consume), mass producibility (made by the
  thousand). NYT's "pig slop" line: built to maximize time spent while minimizing cost.
- It is everywhere now (Examples, via Wikipedia):
  - Social: Facebook "Shrimp Jesus" engagement-bait; a Kenyan creator literally prompted "WRITE ME
    10 PROMPT picture OF JESUS WHICH WILLING BRING HIGH ENGAGEMENT ON FACEBOOK."
  - Kids: an NYT March 2026 investigation found ~40% of videos recommended to children look like AI
    slop (e.g. oddly proportioned horses hatching from eggs), often pretending to be educational.
  - Ads: Coca-Cola's 2024 holiday AI ad misspelled its own brand as "Coca-Coola"; McDonald's
    Netherlands and a Svedka Super Bowl LX 2026 robot ad got the same backlash.
  - Books: AI mushroom-foraging guides on Amazon with wrong images (experts: misidentification can
    be life or death); fake AI novels under real authors' names.
  - Music: Deezer estimated ~70% of streams of AI tracks were fraudulent; the AI "band" Velvet
    Sundown passed 1M Spotify listeners; a man was charged over ~$10M in AI-song royalty fraud.
  - Games: Call of Duty's six-fingered "Necroclaus" zombie Santa; ~20% of 2025 Steam games carry an
    AI-content disclosure ("AI shovelware").
  - Work: HBR/Stanford/BetterUp "workslop" - AI-polished nothing that makes a colleague waste ~2 hours.
  - Events: the Glasgow "Willy's Chocolate Experience" and a fake Dublin Halloween parade drew real
    crowds from AI-generated listings.

## Why It Keeps Happening

The incentive chain (Inference, grounded in the sources). This is the heart of the video:

1. Cost collapsed. Generative AI made text, images, video, and voices almost free to produce.
2. Platforms pay attention, not quality. Feeds rank engagement (clicks, watch time), not effort or truth.
3. So the smart move is to flood. Post huge volumes of cheap content; you only need a few hits.
4. Distribution is blind. Recommendation systems cannot reliably tell slop from real, so they serve it.
5. The money rewards volume. Ad networks (e.g. DoubleVerify's 200+ "AutoBait" sites) and streaming
   royalties (Deezer) pay out, which funds more slop.
6. The loop tightens, and it can poison itself. When AI trains on AI output, quality degrades
   ("model collapse" - e.g. the same invented "Elias Thorne, lighthouse keeper" story reappearing).

Honest line to hold (Open/guardrail): not all AI content is slop, and a curator (art critic
Francesco D'Isa) even argues most human output was always forgettable too. The point is the
incentive, not the tool. And we use the observable "leaner" Dead Internet idea, never the
government-gaslighting conspiracy version.

- Do NOT state "X% of the internet / TikTok / YouTube is AI slop" as settled fact. (The "60% of
  TikTok, 21% of YouTube" figure seen in news headlines is unverified here - see Open Questions.)
- Do NOT say "all AI content is slop" or "AI ruined the internet by itself." (Off-thesis and unfair.)
- Do NOT endorse the Dead Internet conspiracy (secret government plot). (Reject.)
- Do NOT republish real people's AI "slop drop" images (e.g. political AI images) to mock a person;
  reference the phenomenon and use WIT caricature instead.
- Do NOT assert exact dollar/stream figures without attributing them to the named source (Deezer's
  ~70%, the ~$10M fraud case, workslop's ~2 hours) - attribute, do not float as universal truth.

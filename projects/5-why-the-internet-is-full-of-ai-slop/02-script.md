## Draft Strategy

- Core thesis: The internet is filling with cheap AI "slop" because platforms pay for attention,
  not quality, so flooding wins. The villain is the incentive, not AI itself.
- Recurring motif: WIT's phone feed as a rising flood of grey sludge, fed by a conveyor-belt
  machine (prompt in -> sludge out -> coins). WIT scrolls, then slowly sinks into it.
- WIT arc: scrolling happily -> suspicious squint -> inspecting the tells -> unimpressed at the
  machine -> drowning while holding one real photo -> pointing at the real culprit -> tired but clear.
- Script risk: turning into AI-panic or a conspiracy rant. Mitigation: the honest turn (not all AI
  is slop; reject the dead-internet conspiracy), and aim every joke at the system, never a person.

## Section 1: Hook: Is Any Of This Real?

Estimated time: `0:00-0:33`

Words: `103`

Purpose: Open inside a curiosity gap and make the title promise visible fast.

Visual goal: WIT scrolling a phone feed. A too-perfect AI image slides up ("Shrimp Jesus"). WIT squints. Pull back: the whole feed is grey sludge. Title word `SLOP` / `GARBAGE` lands by ~10s.

Narration:

```text
Quick question. Pick up your phone, open any feed, and scroll for ten seconds. [beat] Now be honest: how much of that was actually made by a human?

Three years ago, the answer was easy. All of it.

Today? [deadpan] Good luck.

Because somewhere in that feed there is a photo of a shrimp shaped like Jesus, a news story about an event that never happened, and a hit song by a band that does not exist.

The internet is filling up with garbage. Cheap, fake, mass-produced garbage. And the strange part is, nobody told it to.

Let's find out why.
```

Approval check:

- First spoken line is a curiosity gap, not a scene-setter.
- Topic (a feed full of fakes) is clear by ~3s; contradiction (how much is real?) by ~5s; WIT squint by ~8s; the "garbage/slop" promise by ~10s.
- "Shrimp Jesus" is the hero hook image (license-clear on Commons).

Voice revision notes:

- `[beat]` before "Now be honest"; small `[deadpan]` on "Good luck."

## Section 2: It Has A Name: Slop

Estimated time: `0:33-1:14`

Words: `118`

Purpose: Name the thing and reframe away from AI-panic toward the real cause.

Visual goal: The word `SLOP` stamped over the feed; a pig trough of grey mush gag. A crossed-out "AI = EVIL ROBOTS" board; a circled "ATTENTION = PAID" board.

Narration:

```text
This stuff actually has a name now. We call it slop.

[beat] Slop. Like the grey mush you pour into a pig trough. And that is not an insult I invented - "slop" was literally named Word of the Year in 2025.

Now, one thing to get straight right away. This is not a video about how AI is evil and the robots are coming. [deadpan] That's a different video.

This is about something quieter, and honestly dumber. The internet did not get worse because of some master plan. It got worse because making fake content became almost free - and the internet pays you for attention, not for quality.

So a flood started. And it is still rising.
```

Approval check:

- "Slop" is glossed once (pig trough) so A2 viewers are not lost.
- The reframe replaces "AI is evil" with "paid for attention, not quality."
- Sets up the flood motif for later.

Voice revision notes:

- `[beat]` before the second "Slop"; `[deadpan]` on "That's a different video."

## Section 3: What Slop Actually Is

Estimated time: `1:14-2:08`

Words: `158`

Purpose: Give a clean, learner-friendly 3-part definition and show the tells.

Visual goal: A magnifying glass over a "perfect" AI image. Three handwritten cards drop in: `LOOKS FINE`, `COSTS THEM NOTHING`, `MADE BY THE THOUSAND`. Red circles on six fingers and on "Coca-Coola."

Narration:

```text
So what actually counts as slop? Three things.

One: it looks fine for half a second. Quick glance, looks normal. Look closer, and it falls apart. The hand has six fingers. The words are gibberish. A giant brand spends millions on a holiday ad and misspells its own name as "Coca-Coola." [beat] That really happened.

Two: it costs the maker almost nothing, and it costs you everything. Seconds to make. But it eats your time, your attention, and your trust.

Three: it is made by the thousand. Not one fake post - ten thousand fake posts, sprayed into the feed like a fire hose.

Looks fine, costs them nothing, made by the thousand. [emphasis] That is slop.

A machine pretending to be a person, at full volume.
```

Approval check:

- The 3 marks map to the research definition (superficial competence, asymmetric effort, mass producibility) in plain English.
- "Coca-Coola" and six fingers are concrete, boardable tells.
- Final line restates the motif (machine pretending to be a person).

Voice revision notes:

- `[beat]` before "That really happened"; `[emphasis]` on "That is slop."

## Section 4: The Machine That Feeds Itself

Estimated time: `2:08-3:12`

Words: `196`

Purpose: Explain the incentive chain step by step - the heart of the video.

Visual goal: The conveyor-belt machine. Five numbered steps light up one at a time: a prompt drops in -> grey sludge pours out -> a feed -> a blind algorithm robot -> coins -> back to the prompt. The loop closes.

Narration:

```text
Now the important part. Why does the flood keep coming? Follow the money - slowly, because this is the whole video.

Step one. Making content used to be expensive. You needed a writer, a camera, a musician, time. Now you need a prompt and ten seconds. The cost dropped to almost zero.

Step two. The platform does not pay you for being good. It pays you for engagement - and engagement just means clicks, likes, and time spent. [slower] It rewards attention, not quality.

Step three. So the smart move is not to make one great thing. The smart move is to flood the zone - post a thousand cheap things, and hope a few go viral.

Step four. The algorithm that decides what you see? It cannot really tell slop from real. So it serves the slop anyway.

Step five. The slop makes money - which pays for more slop. [beat] The machine feeds itself.

That is the whole engine. Cheap to make, paid by attention, impossible to filter. Of course it floods.
```

Approval check:

- Each step is spoken in order, learner-followable by ear.
- "Engagement" is glossed inline (clicks, likes, time spent).
- The loop ("the machine feeds itself") sets up the model-collapse idea without jargon.

Voice revision notes:

- `[slower]` on "It rewards attention, not quality"; `[beat]` before "The machine feeds itself."

## Section 5: It Already Got Out

Estimated time: `3:12-4:12`

Purpose: Show the spread is wide and already in serious places.

Visual goal: A rising flood of grey; WIT sinking while holding one real photo above the water. Four tiles surface in turn: a fake Spotify band, a mushroom book with a red `DANGER` mark, a kids-video thumbnail with a six-legged horse, an office inbox.

Narration:

```text
And it is not just funny Facebook pictures anymore. It got out.

Music. A band called the Velvet Sundown passed one million listeners on Spotify before people noticed that not a single member of the band was real.

Books. People are selling AI mushroom-picking guides with fake photos. Pick the wrong mushroom because a robot drew it, and [beat] that one can actually kill you.

Kids. One investigation found that around forty percent of the videos recommended to children looked like AI slop. Forty out of every hundred - nonsense cartoons with six-legged horses, all pretending to teach the alphabet.

Even your job. There is now a word for the co-worker who sends you a beautiful AI document that says absolutely nothing. You just got "workslopped." On average, it costs the person who receives it about two hours to clean up.

[deadpan] So congratulations. The slop is in your feed, your music, your kid's tablet, and your inbox.
```

Approval check:

- Numbers are spoken aloud ("one million," "forty percent / forty out of every hundred," "two hours") per learner-clarity rule.
- Each example is attributed softly to the research (no invented stats).
- WIT is drowning here - the motif peaks.

## Claim Safety Notes

### Safe Claims

- "Slop" was named Word of the Year in 2025 (Merriam-Webster + American Dialect Society).
- AI slop is defined by low effort, low quality/meaning, and high volume made to farm attention/money
  (Kommers et al. Jan 2026: superficial competence, asymmetric effort, mass producibility).
- Coca-Cola's 2024 AI holiday ad misspelled its brand as "Coca-Coola."
- The "Velvet Sundown" AI band passed ~1M Spotify listeners before being widely identified as AI.
- AI mushroom-foraging books with inaccurate images are sold online; misidentification can be deadly
  (New York Mycological Society warning).
- An NYT March 2026 investigation found ~40% of videos recommended to children looked like AI slop.
- "Workslop" study (HBR + Stanford + BetterUp): ~40% of surveyed employees received it; ~2 hours each
  to resolve. (Spoken softly as "on average... about two hours.")
- "Slop" = Word of the Year framing and the pig-trough image (NYT "pig slop" analogy).

### Inferences Used Carefully

- "The algorithm cannot really tell slop from real" - broadly supported; framed plainly, not as a lab fact.
- "The machine feeds itself" - the model-collapse / money-loop idea, kept as a general pattern, no jargon.
- "Making content dropped to almost zero" - true in direction; kept qualitative ("almost free"), no figure.

### Claims Avoided

- No "X% of the internet / TikTok / YouTube is slop" as settled fact (the 60%/21% headline is unverified;
  only the attributed ~40%-kids and study figures are used).
- No claim that all AI content is slop, or that AI alone ruined the internet.
- No endorsement of the dead-internet conspiracy (secret government plot) - it is named and rejected.
- No republishing of real people's AI "slop drop" images to mock a person; examples stay system-level.
- No accusation that any named brand acted in bad faith beyond the documented, sourced facts.

### Humor Safety

- All cheeky lines are aimed at the system/the machine/the incentive ("you cannot arrest an incentive,"
  "it's dumber than that," "congratulations... workslopped"), never at a person, group, or protected category.
- Strongest word used is "garbage/junk" (mild); none in the first 7 seconds, the title, or the thumbnail.
- No dark jokes targeting real harm; the "mushroom can kill you" line is a real safety warning, not a joke.
- The Section 8 CTA is short and straightforward (like/share/subscribe, one brief reason); it never guilt-trips or begs, and the hook / title / thumbnail / strongest words still carry no CTA.

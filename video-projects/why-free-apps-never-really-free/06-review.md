- Version: `prototype-first-45s`

- Runtime: `45s`
- File: [prototype-first-45s.mp4](C:\ME\THINGS\Build a Channel\video-projects\why-free-apps-never-really-free\renders\prototype-first-45s.mp4)
- What works:
  - The core visual identity is usable: warm background, bold text, simple 2D shapes, and orange/teal accents fit `Why It Works`.
  - The hidden invoice metaphor is clear.
  - Wit reads quickly as a dry, deadpan audience surrogate.
  - Text is large and readable at normal YouTube size.
  - The layout feels clean and not cluttered.
- What feels weak:
  - The prototype holds the same composition for too long. Sampled frames around `0s`, `10s`, and `20s` are visually almost identical, and frames around `25s`, `35s`, and `40s` are also almost identical.
  - The first `5-8s` does not yet feel like a strong hook because the visual is already in its settled state instead of building the idea beat by beat.
  - It still feels closer to a polished slide deck than an animated explainer.
  - Wit is present, but he does not do enough micro-actions to land the jokes.
  - The receipt tie labels are a good idea, but they appear too early as a full stack instead of growing as the hidden costs are revealed.
- Fixes needed:
  - Rebuild the first `45s` with more micro-beats before expanding to the full rough cut.
  - Start with fewer elements on screen, then introduce phone, app boxes, Wit, streak warning, hidden invoice, and reframe cards sequentially.
  - Add small motion every `2-4s`: box drops, notification shake, Wit eye/pose change, receipt print, invoice peek, split-screen reveal.
  - Delay the full receipt-tie stack. In the opening, print only `?` or `late`; save `ads`, `fees`, `streak`, and `$9` for later sections.
  - Reduce persistent explanatory subhead usage where the narration already explains the point. Prefer short punch labels like `FREE`, `STREAK!`, `invoice pending`, `generous?`, and `checkout moved`.
  - Keep this simple visual language, but push the motion density up before building the full cut.

Review focus:

- Does the hook feel clear within the first `5-8s`?
- Are there enough visual changes, or does it feel like slides?
- Does Wit help the joke without distracting from the explanation?
- Is the on-screen text readable at normal YouTube size?
- Does the dry humor land with the George voice?
- Should the full cut keep this simple visual language or push more motion?

Prototype caveat:
this version reuses the existing full scene voiceovers and cuts at exactly `45s`, so the ending may stop mid-reframe.
Judge the visual direction and pacing, not the final edit point.

## WIT Opening V1 Review

Decision:
`Do not expand v1.`

Issues found:

- Visual beats did not match the voiceover closely enough.
- Test labels such as `WHY IT WORKS` and `HOOK TEST / 10S` made the clip feel like an internal prototype instead of an uploadable video.
- `VIDEO`, `MAPS`, and `CHAT` appeared before the voice said those words.
- WIT pose usage felt random instead of motivated by the scene context.

V2 acceptance criteria:

- No internal test labels on screen.
- App-example labels must appear only when the narration reaches them.
- WIT movement and pose changes must follow the situation.
- Layout should change across the 10s instead of holding one left/right composition.

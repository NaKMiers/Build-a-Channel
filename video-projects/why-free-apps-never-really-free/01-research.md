# 01 Research

## Simple Thesis

Free apps are usually free at the point of entry, not free in the full economic sense.

The payment moves into:

- attention
- ads
- behavior
- habits
- premium upgrades
- subscriptions
- lock-in
- convenience dependence

Core thesis:

`Free is not always the absence of payment. Sometimes it is just the removal of friction.`

## What People Think

- If an app costs `$0`, it is free.
- Ads are just an annoying side effect.
- Premium features are optional.
- If no money leaves the bank account, nothing serious was spent.

## What Is Actually Happening

Free is often a business strategy.

The app removes the first barrier so more people enter.
Once people enter, the business can monetize later through another mechanism.

## Explanation Chunks

1. Free removes friction.
2. Ads turn attention into inventory.
3. Habit makes users more valuable.
4. Freemium creates a pain gate.
5. Lock-in makes leaving expensive.

## Examples

- YouTube: free video, ads, Premium.
- TikTok/Instagram: free feed, attention and behavior.
- Duolingo: free learning, streak pressure, paid smoother experience.
- Mobile games: free start, paid boosts, lives, gems, skips.
- Cloud storage/productivity apps: free tier, paid once your files or projects matter.

## Analogies

- The invoice is late.
- The checkout moved deeper into the building.
- The app is not cheap. It is patient.

## What Not To Say

- `All free apps are scams.`
- `Every app steals your data.`
- `Big tech is evil.`
- `You should delete everything.`
- `If the product is free, you are the product` as the entire thesis.

## Final Viewer Takeaway

`Free does not mean nobody pays. It means the business chose a different place, time, or method to collect payment.`

## Section Goal

Separate `real work` from `visible work`.

This section should correct the possible misunderstanding from the title:
the video is not accusing people of being lazy.
It is explaining a stranger social reward system where visible busyness is easier to recognize than real progress.

Use the accepted Section 1 style:

```text
static real-life board -> WIT reaction -> short handwritten label -> hard cut
```

No transition overlays.
No word-by-word pop-ins.
No busy dashboard collage.

## Narration

```text
This video is not about lazy people pretending to work. That is a different problem.

This is about something stranger. Modern life often rewards the appearance of work more than the work itself.

Because real progress is hard to see. But busyness is very visible.

You can see meetings. You can see messages. You can see someone typing very fast with a serious face.

You cannot always see thinking. Which is unfortunate, because thinking is where a lot of the actual work happens.
```

## Visual Direction

Use real-life images as evidence, not decoration:

- desk/laptop photo for the correction board
- calendar or meeting notes photo for `appearance of work`
- calm notebook/desk photo for hidden progress and thinking
- keyboard or phone/laptop photo for visible busy signals

Use generated or self-made support assets only where they clarify the joke:

- crossed-out `LAZY?` sticky note
- tiny `different problem` folder
- half-hidden `REAL PROGRESS` paper
- simple evidence tags

Avoid:

- real app logos
- real private messages
- overfilled inbox/calendar dashboards
- making thinking look like sleeping or doing nothing
- visually accusing the viewer of being dishonest

## WIT Role

WIT should not look lazy in this section.

WIT's emotional path:

1. corrective / slightly defensive
2. polite dismissal of the wrong topic
3. skeptical at `APPEARANCE > WORK?`
4. searching for hidden progress
5. overwhelmed by visible busy signals
6. fake serious typing face
7. calm thinking, finally doing the real work

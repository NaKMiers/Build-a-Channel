# Visual Humor Patterns

This is the reusable visual humor catalog for `Why It Works`.

Use these patterns to make simple boards funny without needing complex animation. They are channel-wide tools, not per-video board plans.

## Humor Rule

`The joke should make the explanation clearer.`

If a visual gag makes the board harder to understand, cut it or make it smaller.

## 1. Red Cross-Out

Use when:

- the obvious meaning is wrong
- the narrator reframes the topic
- a marketing word needs to be corrected

Pattern:

```text
FREE
red cross-out
LATER
```

Good labels:

- `not free`
- `not a gift`
- `pay later`
- `nice try`

Avoid:

- crossing out so much text that the frame becomes messy
- using red correction on every board until it loses force

## 2. Bad Arrow

Use when:

- a process is fake-simple
- a company explanation skips the painful part
- a system pretends one step caused another

Pattern:

```text
happy app -> your attention -> money
```

Make the arrow slightly suspicious, too long, too direct, or pointing at the wrong emotional truth.

Good labels:

- `somehow`
- `totally normal`
- `business magic`
- `definitely fine`

## 3. Fake Diagram

Use when:

- the topic has incentives, loops, or hidden mechanisms
- a spoken explanation needs a simple structure
- a dense idea needs a funny map

Pattern:

```text
YOU -> APP -> ADS -> MONEY
        ^       |
        |_______|
```

Rules:

- three nodes is usually enough
- one funny label is better than five accurate labels
- the diagram should be readable when paused

## 4. Real Object With Stupid Label

Use when:

- a real-life object proves the idea
- the topic needs lived-in texture
- a frame feels too abstract

Pattern:

```text
receipt photo or mockup
label: "small financial attack"
```

Good objects:

- receipt
- bill
- phone
- notebook
- checkout page
- calendar

Avoid:

- unclear source rights
- real personal data
- decoration-only objects

## 5. WIT Physically Suffering

Use when:

- the system has a human consequence
- the narration lands a pain point
- the viewer should feel "that is me"

Pattern:

```text
WIT under receipts
label: "monthly pain"
```

Good suffering:

- buried in subscriptions
- attacked by notifications
- trapped in an app screen
- holding a giant receipt
- pulled by a checkout button

Avoid:

- suffering before the script reveals the problem
- suffering that is too dramatic for a small point
- WIT reacting with the wrong emotion

## 6. Object Behaving Like A Person

Use when:

- a thing is acting like it has motives
- a system feels absurdly aggressive
- the joke can be shown without a big scene

Pattern:

```text
receipt sweating
phone whispering "open me"
subscription button smiling
```

Good objects:

- phone
- bill
- app icon
- shopping cart
- notification
- receipt printer

Keep faces simple and deadpan.

## 7. Hidden Thing Revealed Behind Clean Thing

Use when:

- a nice promise hides the real business
- the topic has a "behind the scenes" mechanism
- a clean label needs a darker second layer

Pattern:

```text
front: FREE
behind: ads, habits, data, lock-in
```

Good reveals:

- peel-away label
- opened box
- curtain pulled back
- phone screen cracked open
- fake clean dashboard with messy backside

## 8. List That Gets More Absurd

Use when:

- the narration lists examples
- the point is accumulation
- the joke comes from escalation

Pattern:

```text
costs:
- money
- time
- attention
- small piece of soul
```

Rules:

- keep it short
- make the last item the joke
- do not use obscure cultural references

## 9. Tiny Legal Footnote

Use when:

- a promise has a catch
- the label is too clean to be trusted
- the joke is about hidden conditions

Pattern:

```text
FREE*
*free means we own your Tuesday
```

Rules:

- footnote must still be readable
- the main label carries the frame
- use sparingly for stronger punch

## 10. Before/After Contradiction

Use when:

- the viewer needs to see the cost of a system
- the idea is about transformation
- the contrast is stronger than an explanation

Pattern:

```text
before: "quick free app"
after: "12 notifications and a subscription"
```

Good contrasts:

- before install / after install
- before payday / after bills
- before convenience / after fees
- before productivity video / after same life

## 11. Suspicious Asterisk

Use when:

- one word is too clean
- a promise sounds too generous
- the joke depends on hidden terms

Pattern:

```text
FREE*
*emotionally expensive
```

Use it as a recurring channel gesture, especially for money, internet, and modern-life promises.

## 12. Impossible Receipt

Use when:

- the topic charges people in non-money ways
- the hidden cost is abstract
- WIT needs a physical prop

Pattern:

```text
receipt:
attention      2 hours
patience       gone
self-control   pending
```

Good for:

- free apps
- convenience
- subscriptions
- productivity content
- online shopping

## 13. Progress Bar Of Bad Decisions

Use when:

- a behavior slowly traps the viewer
- the system works by tiny steps
- the joke is gradual loss of control

Pattern:

```text
becoming the product [######----] 64%
```

Keep the bar readable and make the label the joke.

## 14. Too-Clean Marketing Label

Use when:

- a business word hides a normal human pain
- corporate language needs translation
- the script contrasts polite language with reality

Pattern:

```text
"engagement"
red translation: "you cannot leave"
```

Good translations:

- `engagement` -> `you stayed too long`
- `frictionless` -> `too easy to spend`
- `premium` -> `paywall`
- `personalized` -> `we watched`

## Pattern Selection Checklist

Before choosing a pattern, ask:

- Is the point a correction, mechanism, consequence, or payoff?
- Would WIT or a real-life object make it clearer?
- Can the joke be understood by an English learner from context?
- Can the frame be read on a phone?
- Does the visual add a second layer beyond the narration?

## Density Rule

Use one main humor pattern per board.

Two patterns can work only when one is dominant and the other is small, such as:

```text
FREE*
red cross-out
tiny footnote: "*not emotionally"
```

If every element is trying to be the joke, nothing is the joke.

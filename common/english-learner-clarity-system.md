# English Learner Clarity System

Classification: `Core`

Scope: `CHANNEL_WIDE`

Purpose:
make `Why It Works` clear for intermediate English learners without turning the channel into an English class.

This system applies to future scripts, boards, narration tests, rough cuts, and final reviews. It does not audit or rewrite any existing video by itself.

## Core Rule

`Teach the topic first. Make the English learner-friendly by design.`

The channel should feel like:

`interesting English-native YouTube that an intermediate learner can actually follow`

not:

`a vocabulary lesson`

and not:

`a native-speaker meme video that only works if the viewer already understands every slang reference.`

## Product Standard

English learner clarity is a product feature.

Every future video should help the viewer:

- understand the topic on first watch
- remember the main phrase or idea
- follow the joke from the visible situation
- hear key terms clearly
- leave with better real-world English without feeling like they studied

## What This Protects

The channel can borrow roughness, deadpan timing, and visual jokes from creator references, but it should not copy the most native-speaker-dependent density.

Protect against:

- fast abstract explanation
- stacked idioms
- unexplained acronyms
- sarcasm with no visual support
- cultural references that carry the whole joke
- tiny labels during dense narration
- visual chaos that makes the English harder to follow

## Script Standard

Use:

- short sentences
- common words before fancy words
- one clear idea per paragraph
- concrete examples from daily life
- section signposts before topic turns
- repeated key phrases
- jokes that work from the visible situation

Avoid:

- long abstract sentences
- too many idioms in one section
- native-only meme references
- unexplained acronyms
- sarcasm where the literal sentence hides the real meaning
- sudden pivots without a bridge

Working rule:

`The viewer should understand the point even if they miss one joke.`

## Visual Standard

For English learners, visuals carry meaning.

Every important board should make the spoken idea easier to understand through:

- one main idea
- one short label
- one visible object or situation
- clear WIT emotion when WIT appears
- arrows, circles, cross-outs, or red markup that point to the actual idea
- repeated symbols for repeated concepts

Working rule:

`A paused frame should help the viewer recover the meaning.`

## Voice And Pacing Standard

Narration should be clear before it is stylish.

Use:

- moderate speed
- clean pronunciation on key terms
- pauses before dense ideas
- silence or near-silence before some punchlines
- enough time for the viewer to read short labels

Do not speed up just to hit runtime.

Before full voice generation, test the first `45-60` seconds using the channel voice protocol and check whether the pacing still works for a learner who is slightly behind the narrator.

## Useful Phrase Layer

Each video may highlight `3-5` useful phrases naturally.

The phrase layer should:

- help the topic
- help the viewer remember the explanation
- be useful outside the video
- appear on screen where it supports the joke, structure, or payoff
- repeat naturally in different sections

The phrase layer should not:

- interrupt the video for vocabulary teaching
- define obvious words
- overload the viewer with too many phrases
- make the video feel like school

## Humor Standard

Good learner-friendly humor usually comes from:

- visible contradiction
- simple words
- WIT's obvious reaction
- a repeated phrase with a twist
- a real-life object being labeled honestly
- a fake-serious system name for normal human pain

Risky humor usually depends on:

- wordplay only
- slang only
- sarcasm only
- niche cultural references
- too many ideas inside one sentence
- a joke that hides the explanation

Working rule:

`Make the joke visible before making it clever.`

## Required Workflow Placement

Use this system during:

1. topic selection, to reject ideas that need too much hidden cultural context
2. research, to collect concrete examples instead of abstract explanation only
3. script drafting, to keep sections simple and repeated
4. voice revision, to remove density and mark pauses
5. board planning, to make meaning visible
6. voice testing, to check learner pace before full generation
7. rough-cut review, to test subtitles, mobile readability, and paused frames
8. final review, to confirm the video is entertaining but still easy to follow

## Acceptance Criteria

English learner clarity passes when:

- the main topic is understandable on first watch
- the final insight is easy to repeat in one sentence
- key phrases appear naturally and are not over-taught
- on-screen labels support listening
- jokes do not hide the explanation
- WIT reactions make the emotional meaning clear
- dense ideas have breathing room
- the video still feels like entertainment, not a class

## Hard Fails

A future video fails learner clarity if:

- the explanation is correct but sounds like a fast native-speaker essay
- the main joke requires a hidden cultural reference
- the viewer must understand sarcasm to understand the point
- visual labels are too tiny or too fast to read
- key terms are introduced once and never repeated
- the final lesson is difficult to say back simply

## Do Not Do

- Do not make the channel a grammar lesson.
- Do not pause to explain every joke.
- Do not remove personality for clarity.
- Do not use advanced vocabulary just to sound smart.
- Do not copy native-speaker sarcasm if learners cannot decode it from visuals.
- Do not treat captions as a replacement for clear narration and readable boards.
